# Proposed amendments to 7.35.3.18 and 7.35.3.19 NMAC

**Source text amended:** proposed rule 7.35.3 NMAC as published July 23, 2026, `docs/documents/rules-draft-2026-07-23-published.pdf`. Section 7.35.3.18 appears at pages 11 to 12; section 7.35.3.19 at pages 12 to 13.

**Rule hearing:** August 28, 2026.

**Scope of this file:** 7.35.3.18 and 7.35.3.19 only. The provisions these two sections depend on are drafted in `blocking-defects.md`: the conforming amendments to 7.35.3.14 and 7.35.3.20 (H)(5), and the new section 7.35.3.29 on which Subsection D of 7.35.3.19 depends. Amendments outside those provisions are listed as flags, not drafted.

## Conventions

**Redline key.** **Bold** is inserted text. ~~Strikethrough~~ is deleted text. Unmarked text is carried forward from the published rule without change. Subsection headings that are entirely new carry the tag `[NEW]`.

**Quotation of published text.** Text reproduced from the published rule is verbatim, with line breaks introduced by the PDF collapsed to single spaces and no other alteration. Where the published text contains a defect of punctuation or grammar that this file corrects, the correction is marked as an insertion or deletion like any other change.

**Permit-title variable.** The permit title is not written literally anywhere in this file. Six tokens are used instead:

| Token | Current value | Where it appears in this file |
|---|---|---|
| `{{PT}}` | practitioner | lowercase singular, mid-sentence |
| `{{PTS}}` | practitioners | lowercase plural, mid-sentence |
| `{{PT_C}}` | Practitioner | capitalized singular, at the start of a subsection heading |
| `{{PTS_C}}` | Practitioners | capitalized plural, at the start of a sentence |
| `{{PT_UC}}` | PRACTITIONER | uppercase singular, in an NMAC section heading |
| `{{PTS_UC}}` | PRACTITIONERS | uppercase plural, in an NMAC section heading |

The rendered file `7.35.3.18-19-redline-rendered.md` is generated from this file by `tools/render-permit-title.py`, which substitutes the six tokens in one pass. To change the permit title, change the six values at the top of that script and re-run it. Do not edit the rendered file directly. Redline blocks carry the tokens, because the proposed text is what a title change is meant to reach. Quotations do not: where a quotation of the published rule or of a transcript contains the permit title, the title is reproduced literally as it appears in the source.

The variable reaches only 7.35.3.18, 7.35.3.19, and the proposed text in `blocking-defects.md`. It does not reach the rest of 7.35.3 NMAC, and it does not reach the definition of "practitioner" in 7.35.2.7 NMAC, which 7.35.3.7 NMAC incorporates by reference: "The definitions in 7.35.2.7 NMAC apply to this part." (7.35.3.7, page 1.) A change of permit title requires a separate amendment to 7.35.2.7 NMAC, which is a different part of Title 7 and is outside the scope of this file.

Total program hours rise for both permit types. Practicum hours fall; module hours and consultation hours rise by more than the practicum falls. No total is reduced.

**The 84 is inclusive of the simulated patient experience.** This was confirmed by the author of the July 17 recommendation and is the reading adopted here. The curriculum table in that document carries no simulated-patient row, and the published rule states the 5 hours alongside the module hours at 7.35.3.18 C, page 11. So the 84 comprises 79 didactic hours and the 5-hour simulated patient experience. Subsection H states this in one place, which the published rule does not do for any figure.

Three arithmetic notes.

First, the published rule states no hour minimum for the New Mexico educational module at 7.35.3.18 A, page 11, although every other module in that section carries one. The published totals of 150 and 170 therefore understate the published requirement by the length of a module that does not yet exist. Setting that minimum at 8 hours, as this file proposes, makes the total determinate for the first time.

Second, 7.35.3.19 C, page 13, requires {{PTS}} to complete "an additional minimum of 20 hours as a practitioner supervising facilitators during in-person administration day sessions" without saying what the 20 hours are additional to. Read as included within the 120, the published {{PT}} practicum is 100 plus 20, which is the 120 stated in Subsection A, and the published {{PT}} program total is 170. Read as on top of the 120, the {{PT}} practicum is 140 and the program total is 190. The proposed 194 exceeds the published total on either reading. Paragraph C as amended states which reading governs.

Third, the second reading is what fixes the floor under the practicum. For the {{PT}} total to exceed 190, the practicum and the consultation must together exceed 106 hours. The proposal is 90 plus 20, which is 110, so the margin against the second reading is four hours. That margin is why the practicum is drafted at 90 rather than at the 72 in the July 17 recommendation: 72 plus 20 is 92, which falls 14 hours short of the published requirement on that reading. The sensitivity is set out in `metz-crosswalk.md`, Part 6.3.

# 7.35.3.18 EDUCATIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR CERTIFYING CLINICIANS, {{PTS_UC}}, AND FACILITATORS

## Subsection A. New Mexico educational module

> **A.** Requirements for certifying clinician, {{PT}}, and facilitator certification: All certifying clinicians, {{PTS}}, and facilitators who provide medical psilocybin services, including all such providers who are certified by the department on the basis of having completed an educational program from another jurisdiction, shall complete a New Mexico educational module created or approved by the department**, consisting of a minimum of eight didactic hours,** prior to applying for certification, which shall include at a minimum:
>
> (1) Education on the New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Act;
> (2) An overview of the use of psilocybin in a medical and therapeutic model in New Mexico;
> (3) Education on the social, cultural and historical aspects of psilocybin use, including traditional use by Native American and other populations in New Mexico;
> (4) Spiritual, existential, religious, and theological (SERT) aspects of psilocybin therapy; and
> (5) Evaluation to demonstrate competency in the above areas.

**Effect.** Supplies the missing hour minimum. The New Mexico module is the only module in 7.35.3.18 with no hour count, and it gates every certification pathway, including the out-of-jurisdiction pathway. Eight hours matches the certifying clinician module in Subsection B and sits inside the range of 6 to 8 hours in the July 17 recommendation.

**Consequence for certifying clinicians.** Certifying clinicians take the New Mexico module and the Subsection B module and nothing else in this section. Their didactic requirement becomes 16 hours, from 8 plus an unpriced module. This is the only effect of these amendments on certifying clinicians.

## Subsection C. {{PT_C}} and facilitator psilocybin therapy module

> **C.** Requirements for initial {{PT}} and facilitator certification: All {{PTS}} and facilitators shall complete a psilocybin therapy module consisting of a minimum of ~~30~~ **65** didactic hours and 5 hours of simulated patient experience, prior to applying for certification, which shall include:
>
> (1) Overview of {{PT}}/facilitator responsibilities including how to work as part of a care team;
> (2) 42 CFR part 2;
> (3) Trauma-Informed Care**, a minimum of eight didactic hours;**
> (4) Psychedelic emergencies/urgencies/medical monitoring;
> (5) Psilocybin actions, interactions and pharmacology;
> (6) Set and setting;
> (7) Ethics in therapeutic settings**, a minimum of 14 didactic hours**;
> (8) Patient-centered approaches and care;
> (9) Preparation and integration sessions;
> (10) Administration session;
> (11) Dosing;
> (12) Psychedelic de-escalation techniques**, and recognition of and support for challenging experiences during and after administration sessions, including hallucinogen persisting perception disorder**;
> (13) Non-ordinary states of consciousness;
> (14) Self-care;
> (15) Research and data collection requirements;
> **(16) End-of-life and palliative care, a minimum of 10 didactic hours;**
> **(17) Screening, suicidality, and crisis response, a minimum of five didactic hours;**
> **(18) Substance use disorder, including nicotine use disorder, a minimum of four didactic hours;**
> **(19) Post-traumatic stress disorder: recognition, screening, contraindications, and collaboration with or referral to a provider trained in the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder, a minimum of four didactic hours. This paragraph does not require training in the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder;**
> **(20) Somatic awareness and therapeutic touch, including consent, a minimum of four didactic hours;**
> **(21) History and traditional use of psilocybin and other psychedelics, a minimum of three didactic hours;**
> **(22) Individual and group treatment models and group dynamics;**
> ~~(16)~~ **(23)** A simulated patient experience of no less than 5 hours; and
> ~~(17)~~ **(24)** Evaluation to demonstrate competency in the above areas.

**Effect on hours.** The core module rises from 30 didactic hours to 65, and the module including its 5-hour simulated patient experience rises from 35 to 70. Eight topic areas carry stated minimums: trauma-informed care 8, ethics 14, end-of-life and palliative care 10, screening and suicidality and crisis response 5, substance use disorder 4, post-traumatic stress disorder 4, somatic awareness and therapeutic touch 4, and history and traditional use 3. Those total 52, which leaves 13 of the 65 didactic hours unallocated across the remaining topics. Programs retain discretion over the allocation of those 13 hours and over any hours above the 65-hour floor.

**The ethics figure and its denominator.** Fourteen hours is 20 percent of the 70-hour module, being the 65 didactic hours plus the 5 hours of simulated patient experience. The July 17 recommendation ties ethics to "roughly 20% of didactic hours" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 2) and attributes the proportion to the Colorado stakeholder interviews: "ethics as approximately 20% of total didactic hours" (page 5). It does not say which total. Twenty percent of the 84-hour standard would be about 17. The module is the denominator used here, because the ethics requirement sits inside the module and the New Mexico module and the role-specific modules carry their own separate content lists.

**Effect on the simulated patient hours.** The published lead-in reads "a minimum of 30 didactic hours and 5 hours of simulated patient experience," which does not say whether the 5 hours count toward the 30. The July 9 draft said expressly that they did not: "A simulated patient experience of no less than 5 hours in addition to the 25 didactic hours" (July 9 draft, page 4, quoted at `analysis/july23-published-delta.md`, S48). Subsection H restores that statement and places both figures inside the 84-hour total.

**New topics (16), (17) and (18).** Each was requested on the record at the July 17 afternoon Training and Education Committee meeting and none appears in the published topic list. The committee transcript records the request: "So I think from what's been presented, you know, that would be substance use disorder, end of life, suicidality, PTSD, that that be incorporated into curriculum requirements." Substance use disorder is the subject of an open question in the July 17 recommendation, which asks "whether a dedicated addiction/SUD teaching plan is needed and where it belongs in the curriculum" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 6). Nicotine use disorder is an approved indication, so the four hours are proposed rather than left open.

**Post-traumatic stress disorder, now drafted at (19).** Every qualifying condition in the Medical Psilocybin Act now has required content. The Act lists four at Section 3(I), reproduced in 7.35.2.7 as "Qualifying condition": "major treatment-resistant depression", "posttraumatic stress disorder", "substance use disorders", and "end-of-life care". As published, 7.35.3.18 C addresses none of them by name. As amended, treatment-resistant depression is reached through (17), which the July 17 recommendation frames as screening and suicidality "presented within the context of major and treatment-resistant depression"; substance use disorder through (18); end-of-life care through (16); and post-traumatic stress disorder through (19).

PTSD was requested on the record at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting, alongside the other three: "So I think from what's been presented, you know, that would be substance use disorder, end of life, suicidality, PTSD, that that be incorporated into curriculum requirements."

The four hours are scoped to recognition, screening, contraindications, and collaboration or referral, and the paragraph says in terms that it does not require training in the treatment of PTSD. That is drafted to the objection in the July 17 recommendation, which states that the eight-hour trauma module "is not PTSD treatment training: additional training is needed to treat complex PTSD" and proposes instead that "if PTSD treatment is outside the Licensed Provider's scope of practice, collaboration with a PTSD-trained provider is recommended or required" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 2). A scope-of-practice rule of that kind would amend 7.35.3.13 B and is still not drafted here. What is drafted is the curriculum content that makes such a rule operable.

**For review.** Flagged for the committee's review.

## Subsection H. Total module hours `[NEW]`

> **H. Total module hours: The requirements of Subsections A, C and D of this section shall together total a minimum of 84 hours for a facilitator applicant. The requirements of Subsections A, C and E of this section shall together total a minimum of 84 hours for a {{PT}} applicant. Of that total, no fewer than 79 hours shall be didactic hours, and 5 hours shall be the simulated patient experience required by Subsection C of this section, which is in addition to the didactic hours required by that subsection. The hour minimums stated for individual content areas within Subsection C are floors within the 84-hour total and do not limit an educational program's allocation of the balance of the hours.**

**Effect.** Makes 84 the binding figure and the component minimums subordinate to it. This follows the July 17 recommendation, which states: "Component ranges are illustrative; the binding minimum is the total" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 3). It also makes the aggregate auditable: the published rule states hour counts module by module and nowhere states a total, so no single provision states what the educational requirement is.

**Why 84 rather than 84 plus 5.** The recommendation's curriculum table has no simulated-patient row and its total line reads simply "Total 84" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 3). The author confirmed that the 5 hours sit inside the 84. Subsection H therefore states the 84 as a module total and fixes the didactic floor at 79 inside it, so that a program cannot satisfy the standard by expanding the simulated patient experience.

**For review.** This is a change from an earlier draft of these amendments, which read the 84 as didactic only and set the total at 84 plus 5. Flagged for confirmation at the committee's review.

## Subsection A. Minimum practicum hours; administration day sessions

> **A.** Minimum practicum hours; administration day sessions: An individual who seeks to become certified as a {{PT}} or facilitator shall participate in supervised practice training, otherwise referred to as a "practicum", after completing at least ~~half of the didactic requirements~~ **40 of the didactic hours required by 7.35.3.18 NMAC** and all of the simulated patient requirements of the educational requirements. The practicum shall consist of a minimum of ~~100~~ **80** hours of supervised practice training for facilitators and ~~120~~ **90** hours for {{PTS}} and shall be completed prior to applying for certification. Students shall participate in a minimum of ~~80~~ **60** hours of administration day sessions, where students are provided the opportunity to experience, observe, or conduct supervised facilitation of administration day sessions in-person with a minimum of 14 different patients over a minimum of eight different administration ~~and same-day~~ **day** sessions with the following criteria:
>
> (1) A minimum of six different patients during a minimum of six individual administration day sessions; and
> (2) A minimum of two different group administration day sessions with four or more patients in each group administration day session.

**Effect on hours.** Practicum falls from 100 to 80 for facilitators and from 120 to 90 for {{PTS}}. The reduction is taken entirely from administration day session hours, 80 down to 60, and from the {{PT}} supervision hours in Subsection C, 20 down to 10. The 20 hours of preparation and integration sessions in Subsection B are untouched.

**Effect on patient contact.** None. The minimum of 14 different patients, the minimum of eight different sessions, the six individual sessions with six different patients, and the two group sessions with four or more patients each are all carried forward unchanged. What changes is the number of hours a student must log, not the number of patients a student must see. Sixty hours across eight sessions is 7.5 hours per session.

**Effect on the entry gate.** The published gate reads "after completing at least half of the didactic requirements and all of the simulated patient requirements of the educational requirements." As recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, M2, "half of the didactic requirements" does not say half of what: half of the Subsection C module, or half of the aggregate of Subsections A, C, and D or E. The amendment replaces the fraction with a flat number, which removes the question rather than answering it in a way that has to be recomputed every time an hour figure moves. Forty is just over half of the 79 didactic hours required of either permit type under Subsection H of 7.35.3.18 NMAC as amended.

**Record note on the entry gate.** The gate is new text that appears in neither the July 9 draft nor either July 17 transcript. The question was asked directly at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting: "I was curious at what point is the student eligible to start practicum ... Did they have to have been through a certain amount of didactic first or is it didactic needs to be complete and then they start their practicum?" The answer recorded from the chair was "in full didactic is completed in full before practicum begins." The amendment does not reopen whether the gate should be half or all; it makes the published half determinate.

**Terminology.** "administration and same-day sessions" is corrected to "administration day sessions," which is the term used everywhere else in the section. The published rule uses the older phrase once, at page 13. This is recorded as a drafting note at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, N4.

## Subsection B. Minimum practicum hours; preparation and integration sessions

> **B.** Minimum practicum hours; in-person and integration sessions: Students shall participate in a minimum of 20 hours of in-person preparation and integration sessions, where students are provided the opportunity to experience, observe, or conduct (when licensure allows) preparation or integration sessions. This shall include a minimum of six different patients during individual sessions. This shall include at least one group preparatory and one group integration session with a minimum of four or more patients. **The patients counted under this subsection and the sessions counted under this subsection may be the same patients and the same group cohorts counted under Subsection A of this section.**

**Effect.** Hours unchanged at 20. The added sentence resolves the ambiguity recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, M14: as published, a student must reach either 14 different patients or as many as 28, depending on whether Subsection B's six patients may be Subsection A's six patients. Programs cannot design placements, and the department cannot audit records under 7.35.3.17 C(2)(d), without knowing which. The amendment adopts the lower figure, which is the reading Subsection A's own arithmetic supports: six individual patients plus eight group patients equals the 14 that Subsection A requires.

## Subsection C. {{PT_C}} supervision hours

> **C.** {{PT_C}} supervision hours: {{PTS_C}} shall complete an additional minimum of ~~20~~ **10** hours as a {{PT}} supervising facilitators during in-person administration day sessions, **which hours are included within the 90 hours required by Subsection A of this section and are additional to the hours required of facilitators,** which shall include a minimum of two different patients during individual administration day sessions, and a minimum of one group administration ~~day sessions~~ **day session** with a minimum of four or more patients in the group.

**Effect on hours.** The supervision requirement falls from 20 hours to 10, following the July 17 recommendation: "Supervisory hours, Licensed Providers only (10 hours). ... Reduced from the 20 hours in the 6-12-26 draft" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 4). Patient and session minimums within the supervision hours are unchanged.

**Effect on the ambiguity.** The published "additional minimum of 20 hours" does not say what the 20 hours are additional to. Under the amendment the {{PT}} practicum is 60 plus 20 plus 10, which equals the 90 stated in Subsection A, and no figure in the section is left to inference.

## Subsection D. Practicum sequence `[NEW]`

> **D. Practicum sequence: A student shall complete the 60 hours of administration day sessions required by Subsection A of this section in the following sequence. The hour figures stated in this subsection are minimums within the practicum totals required by Subsection A and do not limit the discretion of a practicum supervisor to assign a student duties of greater responsibility as the student demonstrates proficiency. Hours of preparation and integration sessions are counted under Subsection B of this section and not under this subsection.**
>
> **(1) Stage one, initial supervised experience: a minimum of 20 hours. Stage one shall be completed with practicum participants who are not qualified patients, in accordance with 7.35.3.29 NMAC. If and for so long as 7.35.3.29 NMAC is not in effect, stage one shall consist of a minimum of 20 hours during which the student observes administration day sessions and provides support to the {{PT}} or facilitator conducting them, and the student shall not possess or administer medical psilocybin products during stage one. Preparation and integration sessions are required for each participant. Stage one shall be completed before a training permit is issued under Subsection G of this section.**
> **(2) Stage two, co-facilitation: a minimum of 25 hours during which the student, holding a current training permit issued under Subsection G of this section, serves as the second person of a co-facilitation pair with a certified {{PT}} or facilitator. A practicum supervisor shall assign stage two cases on the basis of the acuity of the patient's presentation and the student's demonstrated proficiency.**
> **(3) Stage three, group practicum: a minimum of 15 hours of direct participation in group administration day sessions.**
> **(4) Stage four applies to {{PT}} applicants only and consists of the supervision hours required by Subsection C of this section.**

**Effect.** Adopts the scaffolded sequence in Recommendation 3 of the July 17 recommendation, with one departure noted below. Arithmetic: 20 plus 25 plus 15 equals the 60 administration day session hours in Subsection A. For {{PT}} applicants, stage four adds 10, and Subsection B adds 20, for the 90 in Subsection A.

**Response to the objection recorded on July 17.** The department stated at the afternoon committee meeting that it had not broken the practicum into blocks, and gave a reason: "the main reason we didn't break it out into separate, like separate sections, like saying, oh, 20 hours for this, 20 hours for that, is because you are going to have different needs depending on the student," and "as you grow proficient, the people who are supervising you actually start giving you those harder and harder cases. And that should really be the situation here as well, where we're not trying to define in an artificial way. Here are separate individual blocks." The lead-in sentence of Subsection D is drafted to that objection. The stages are floors, not caps: a supervisor who judges a student ready may assign co-facilitation duties at any point after the training permit issues, and the hours logged count toward whichever stage minimum is unmet. What the stages fix is the order in which a student may first take on each level of responsibility, and the point at which a training permit is required.

**Stage one and Recommendation 3.** The July 17 recommendation proposes that stage one consist of "Initial facilitation experience with well participants (approximately 30 hours). Two to three sessions as a facilitator in a retreat or peer-support model" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 3). The committee deck of the same date states the same step as "Well participants - ~30 hours  Retreat or peer-support model; supervised exposure to non-ordinary states before clinical work. Prep and integration for every participant. Training permit issues on completion." (slide 4, with the em dash rendered here as a hyphen).

That step is drafted, as a new section 7.35.3.29 NMAC, and Paragraph (1) above is written in two limbs so that the practicum works either way. The reason for the two limbs is that the authority for the step is a statutory question this rule cannot answer. Nothing in 7.35.3 NMAC authorizes administering psilocybin to a person who is not a qualified patient, and 7.35.3.14, page 9, authorizes provision only "to qualified patients." The rule cites its own authority as the Medical Psilocybin Act, Section 26-2D-7 NMSA 1978, and states its objective by reference to Sections 26-2D-1 through -11 NMSA 1978 (7.35.3.3 and 7.35.3.6, page 1). Whether those sections reach administration to a non-patient is not determinable from the rule text and is not answered anywhere in this repository. It is the first of the questions at `open-questions.md`.

The July 17 morning board transcript records the point being made about the statute rather than the rule: "what's going to be presented in the training committee this afternoon is impossible to do adequate training without having the ability for people to use psilocybin in the training," offered in the context of hoping "that there's going to be a fix-it bill." The afternoon committee transcript records the request in program terms: "yes, we need an expansion for practicums to be feasible ... for them to be operated on well patients, those without qualifying conditions."

Drafting 7.35.3.29 with an express contingency puts the ask on the record at the August 28 hearing without asserting an authority the department may not have. If the authority exists or is supplied, the section operates and stage one is the step the recommendation describes. If it does not, the second limb of Paragraph (1) governs and stage one is observation and support, which builds the same risk gradient through role rather than through the clinical status of the participant.

## Subsection E. Practicum location `[was D]`

> ~~D.~~ **E.** Practicum location: All practicum hours shall take place in an approved healing center or other approved location. **A healing center or other approved location may host a practicum student who is not enrolled in an educational program with which the healing center or other approved location is affiliated, provided that the student is supervised by a practicum supervisor. A healing center or other approved location that hosts a practicum student shall not be required to hold certification as a psilocybin educational program by reason of hosting the student.**

**Effect.** Addresses part of the blocking defect recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, B4. The published subsection is identical to the July 9 draft and confines practicum to approved locations without providing any route for a student whose program has no co-located site. The July 17 afternoon committee transcript records the point: "the current draft requires that all hours be held in an approved healing center, which I think can potentially be a bottleneck for rural trainees," and the proposed remedy, "the apprenticeship or the site supervisor model, similar to what they do for counseling licensure or direct entry midwifery."

## Subsection F. Practicum supervisors: WITHDRAWN

A subsection registering practicum supervisors, setting a one-year experience floor, and requiring the department to publish a list of supervisors and available host sites was drafted and has been withdrawn.

**Why.** None of it is traceable to a source. The one-year floor, the registration scheme and the published list appear in neither the July 17 recommendation, nor either July 17 transcript, nor the rule as published. They were written to make Subsection E workable, which makes them invention rather than drafting.

**What survives.** Subsection E, which is traceable: the July 17 recommendation proposes at page 4 that "the committee should consider an apprenticeship or site-supervisor model, similar to counseling licensure or direct-entry midwifery, in which an approved supervisor at an approved location can host practicum students who are not enrolled at a co-located training program." Subsection E does that and leaves vetting where the rule already leaves it, with the department. The published rule already uses the term "practicum supervisor" at 7.35.3.19 E without defining it, so Subsection E introduces no new undefined term.

**The problem it addressed is real and is on the record.** The July 17 afternoon committee transcript records the placement bottleneck: "the current draft requires that all hours be held in an approved healing center, which I think can potentially be a bottleneck for rural trainees." The vetting question was left open by the recommendation at page 6: "If practicum placements are decoupled from training programs through an apprenticeship or site-supervisor model, the committee will need to decide who vets and approves site supervisors and instructors."

**To reinstate.** Say so and it goes back as a new Subsection F ahead of the present F, with the remaining subsections re-lettered again. The text is in the git history of this file at commit c541c14.

## Subsection G. Training permit `[NEW]`

> **G. Training permit:**
>
> **(1) Issuance: The department shall issue a training permit to an applicant who:**
> **(a) is registered as a student with a psilocybin educational program certified under 7.35.3.12 NMAC;**
> **(b) has completed the didactic requirement for entry to the practicum stated in Subsection A of this section;**
> **(c) has completed stage one of the practicum sequence under Subsection D of this section;**
> **(d) submits documentation of current certification or licensure meeting Subsection F of 7.35.3.18 NMAC;**
> **(e) submits an attestation that the applicant is not registered in any jurisdiction as a sex offender; and**
> **(f) submits the applicant's legal name, address, telephone number, e-mail address, signature, and date of application submittal.**
> **(2) Decision: Once the department has received a completed application, it will review the application and render a decision within 30 calendar days. If the department denies an application, the department shall provide notice of the denial within 30 calendar days in accordance with this rule. An applicant whose application is denied may appeal the denial in accordance with this rule.**
> **(3) Authority conferred: A training permit authorizes the permittee to possess and administer medical psilocybin products to qualified patients, and to practicum participants in accordance with 7.35.3.29 NMAC, as provided in 7.35.3.14 NMAC, and to conduct supervised facilitation of administration day sessions and supervised preparation and integration sessions as required by this section, in each case only while under the direct, on-site supervision of a practicum supervisor. A training permittee shall not purchase medical psilocybin products, shall not provide medical psilocybin services other than as part of the practicum or the consultation requirement in Subsection H of this section, and shall not hold themselves out as a certified {{PT}} or facilitator.**
> **(4) Term: A training permit is valid for 24 months from the date of issuance and may be renewed once for a further 12 months. A training permit expires on the earlier of the end of its term, the date the permittee ceases to be registered as a student with a certified psilocybin educational program, or the date the department issues the permittee a {{PT}} or facilitator certification.**
> **(5) Status: A training permittee is a certificant for purposes of this rule.**

**Effect.** Creates the permit that Recommendation 3 of the July 17 recommendation proposes, and supplies the authorization that the blocking defect at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, B1, identifies as missing. The recommendation states the purpose: "On completion of step 1, students should be issued a time-limited training permit authorizing them to practice under supervision," which "gives trainees a lawful pathway to accrue the client contact the practicum and sign-off require" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, page 4).

**Why Paragraph (5) is there.** 7.35.3.22, page 15, provides that program participation "does not relieve the qualified patient or certificant from criminal prosecution or civil penalties for ... Unlawful distribution, transfer, or use of psilocybin." A practicum student is not a certificant under the published rule. Paragraph (5) places the training permittee inside the class the rule already addresses, so that the permit carries both the authorization in 7.35.3.14 and the corresponding limits and discipline provisions.

**Record note.** The department stated at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting that a training permit "really wouldn't be necessary with the model that we have and the way that we put the practitioner aspect in there. Just because that already would be covered as them being a registered student with an educational program where they have that ability to be a part of those training sessions, including to conduct them under supervision," and "those students already will have that kind of protection ability to be there as a part of being registered as a student with an educational program." The provision producing that result does not appear in the published text. 7.35.3.14, page 9, names {{PTS}}, facilitators, and healing center owners and employees, and does not name students. This is set out in full in `blocking-defects.md`.

**Cross-reference.** Subsection H is inoperative without the amendment to 7.35.3.14 in `blocking-defects.md`. The permit is the mechanism; 7.35.3.14 is the provision that performs the authorizing function for every other category of person in the room.

## Subsection H. Supervision and consultation; case presentation sign-off `[NEW]`

> **H. Supervision and consultation; case presentation sign-off:**
>
> **(1) A training permittee shall complete a minimum of 20 hours of supervision or consultation during the training permit period, in addition to the practicum hours required by this section.**
> **(2) Sign-off requires the permittee to present a minimum of two cases of qualified patients with whom the permittee has personally worked in the medical psilocybin program. Each case presentation shall take the form of a biopsychosocial case conceptualization addressing presenting concerns, risk factors, supportive factors, treatment considerations, and recommendations for aftercare.**
> **(3) The supervisor or consultant shall complete an evaluation on a form approved by the department for each case presented, and shall submit it through the electronic system designated by the department.**
> **(4) The 10 hours of mentoring sessions required by Subsection A of 7.35.3.17 NMAC are credited toward the 20 hours required by this subsection.**
> **(5) The requirements of this subsection shall be completed prior to applying for certification.**

**Effect on hours.** Consultation rises from 10 hours to 20. Paragraph (4) prevents double counting by crediting the existing 10 hours of mentoring toward the 20, so the net addition is 10 hours.

**For review.** The July 17 recommendation gives a range, "20-30 total hours." The bottom of the range is drafted. A range stated in a rule operates as its floor in any event, so 20 is what "20 to 30" would enact. The provenance of the range, and the reason the top of it is arguable, are set out at `metz-crosswalk.md`, Part 6.6. Flagged for the committee's review.

**Effect on timing.** Paragraph (5) ties the consultation to certification. Under the published rule the mentoring in 7.35.3.17 A occurs "after graduation and after practicum hours are completed," while 7.35.3.19 A requires the practicum to be "completed prior to applying for certification" and 7.35.3.9 C(3), page 3, does not list mentoring among the items an applicant must document. A graduate may therefore be certified and practising before, or entirely without, the mentoring. This is the gap recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, M11.

**Basis.** Recommendation 4 of the July 17 recommendation: "20-30 total hours of supervision or consultation completed during the training permit period, in addition to the practicum hours above, with sign-off tied to case presentation" (`docs/documents/metz-recommendations-2026-07-17.pdf`, pages 4 and 5). The identified defect in the current requirement was stated at the meeting: "the current drafts, 10 hours of mentoring never require that the permittee has seen a client. So consultation groups in Colorado have allowed completion of these hours by attendance alone." The bottom of the 20 to 30 range is proposed, because a range stated in a rule operates as its floor and 20 is therefore what the range would enact.

**Decision that crosses into the other session's scope.** Paragraph (4) is drafted so that 7.35.3.17 A need not be amended. The alternative is to move the whole requirement into 7.35.3.17 A and delete Paragraph (4). That choice belongs to whoever holds 7.35.3.17. Both options are set out in `blocking-defects.md` under "Flags."

## Subsection I. End-of-life practice `[NEW]`

> **I. End-of-life practice: Before providing medical psilocybin services to a patient enrolled on the basis of end-of-life care other than under the direct supervision of a {{PT}} or facilitator who meets the requirements of this subsection, a {{PT}} or facilitator shall have completed at least one co-facilitated end-of-life case during the practicum and presented at least one end-of-life case under Subsection H of this section.**

The transcript records a request that end-of-life be treated as "a distinct enough set that really does need specific training." Subsection J adds no hours to the totals; it conditions one class of independent practice on experience the student will in most cases already have logged.

## Subsection J. Waiver of practicum requirements `[was F]`

> ~~F.~~ **J.** Waiver of practicum requirements: The department may otherwise waive, temporarily suspend, or reduce the practicum requirements for individuals applying for certification, in order to facilitate the certification of individuals trained by other government-approved programs, and to build the initial infrastructure of the program. **The department shall act on a request under this subsection within 30 calendar days and shall state the basis for its decision in writing. The department shall not under this subsection: (1) reduce an applicant's practicum below 40 hours of contact time with qualified patients; (2) waive the requirements of Subsection H of this section; or (3) authorize any person to possess or administer medical psilocybin products who is not certified or does not hold a current training permit. The department shall publish the number of waivers granted under this subsection and the general grounds for them, at least annually.**

**Effect.** Addresses the second half of the blocking defect at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, B4: the published waiver has no criteria, no procedure, and no time limit, and it is the only relief from a practicum whose supply is bounded by the enrolled patient population. The 40-hour floor is the figure the rule already uses for the two dated waivers, at 7.35.3.10 D(1), page 5, and 7.35.3.19 G(4), page 13. Paragraph (3) prevents the waiver from being used as a substitute for the authorization in 7.35.3.14.

## Subsection K. Waiver of practicum hours requirement for applications received by December 31, 2027 `[was G]`

> ~~G.~~ **K.** Waiver of practicum hours requirement for applications received by December 31, 2027: An applicant for certification shall not be required to satisfy the full New Mexico practicum hours requirement if the applicant:
>
> (1) Applies for certification by December 31, 2027;
> (2) Completes the didactic requirements by December 31, 2027;
> (3) Graduates from an educational program that the department certifies by December 31, 2027 or that the department has included on the department-approved list of educational programs by December 31, 2027; and
> (4) Demonstrates completion of at least 40 hours of contact time through logs or other records of the sessions, including:
> (a) A minimum of two separate individual sessions including the appointments for preparation, administration and integration; and
> (b) ~~A minimum of one group session~~ **A minimum of two separate group sessions** including the appointments for preparation, administration, and integration.

**Effect.** Conforms the group-session floor to 7.35.3.10 D(1), page 5, which requires "a minimum of two separate individual sessions and two separate group sessions" for the same 40 hours. As published the two waivers set different floors for the same evidence, and an applicant who trained out of state and also graduated from a New Mexico certified program falls under both. This is recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, M3. The department described the intent as matching at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting: "this is to match the same thing that we're requiring even in-state up until December 31st of 27 is that 40 contact hours with patients."

**Alignment direction.** This amendment conforms 7.35.3.19 upward to 7.35.3.10 rather than the reverse, because 7.35.3.10 is outside the scope of this file. Conforming 7.35.3.10 downward to one group session would achieve the same consistency and is the alternative; it is flagged in `blocking-defects.md`.

**Not addressed here.** The circularity recorded at `analysis/july23-rule-concerns.md`, M4, is not resolved by this file. Paragraph (3) requires the applicant to graduate from a certified program, and 7.35.3.17 B, page 11, makes completion of the practicum a condition of graduation, so an applicant who satisfies Paragraph (3) may have nothing left to waive. Resolving that requires a change to 7.35.3.17 B or to Paragraph (3), and the choice between them is a decision for whoever holds 7.35.3.17.
