## Answered

**2. Is the 84 inclusive of the 5 simulated-patient hours, or on top of them?** ANSWERED: inclusive.

Drafted accordingly. 7.35.3.18 H now states 84 as a module total with a didactic floor of 79 inside it, so the standard cannot be met by expanding the simulated patient experience instead of the didactic content. Allocation: New Mexico module 8 didactic, core module 65 didactic plus the 5-hour simulated patient experience, role-specific module 6 didactic. This is a change from the earlier draft, which read the 84 as didactic only and totalled 89. **Flagged for confirmation at the committee's review.**

**3, first part. Are the roughly 30 well-participant hours inside the 62 and 72, or additional?** ANSWERED: inside.

Also confirmed: if the program cannot support well participants at this stage, the practicum bucket holds at the same size and the well-participant carve-out is made later rather than the bucket shrinking. That is what 7.35.3.19 D(1) does. It is written in two limbs, so stage one is earned with practicum participants under 7.35.3.29 if that section is in effect, and as observation and support with qualified patients if it is not. Either way the hours stay in the bucket.

**6. Ethics at roughly 20 percent: 20 percent of what?** RESOLVED INTERNALLY as 20 percent of the 70-hour core module, which is 14 hours.

The recommendation says "roughly 20% of didactic hours" (page 2) and attributes the proportion to the Colorado interviews as "ethics as approximately 20% of total didactic hours" (page 5), without saying which total. Twenty percent of the 84-hour standard would be about 17. Fourteen is drafted, on the reasoning that the ethics requirement sits inside the core module and the New Mexico and role-specific modules carry their own content lists. **Worth a sentence of confirmation, not a decision.**

## Open, and each one changes drafting

**1. Step 1 with well participants: were you assuming the statute already permits it, or assuming a fix-it bill?**

This is the largest open item. Recommendation 3 step 1 is "Initial facilitation experience with well participants (approximately 30 hours). Two to three sessions as a facilitator in a retreat or peer-support model" (written draft, page 3; slide 4 adds "Prep and integration for every participant").

Nothing in 7.35.3 NMAC authorizes administering psilocybin to a person who is not a qualified patient. 7.35.3.14, page 9, authorizes provision only "to qualified patients." The July 17 morning board transcript treats it as legislative: "impossible to do adequate training without having the ability for people to use psilocybin in the training," said while hoping "that there's going to be a fix-it bill."

Drafted as new section 7.35.3.29 with an express contingency, with the fallback limb in 7.35.3.19 D(1), so the practicum works either way. **If the answer is that a fix-it bill is needed, the contingency stays and the section is an on-the-record ask at the August 28 hearing. If you read the Act as already permitting it, the contingency comes out.**

**3, second part. Practicum totals: 72 for a Licensed Provider does not clear the published rule on one of its two readings.**

7.35.3.19 C requires "an additional minimum of 20 hours" of supervision without saying additional to what. On the natural reading the published practitioner practicum is 120 and the program is 170. On the other reading it is 140 and the program is 190.

With the module fixed at 84, the practicum and consultation must together exceed 106 to stay above 190:

| Practicum | With 20 consultation hours | With 30 consultation hours |
|---|---|---|
| 72, as recommended | 176 | 186 |
| 76 | 180 | 190 |
| 80 | 184 | 194 |
| 86 | 190 | 200 |
| 90, as drafted | 194 | 204 |

The facilitator side is never at risk; the published total is 150 and the module alone rises by 44. **The question is whether you are content with the practitioner practicum at 90 rather than 72 for this reason, or would rather hold 72 and accept 186 against the 190 reading.** Nothing about the structure changes either way; only the number in 7.35.3.19 A.

**4. The 20 to 30 consultation hours: what is the range doing?**

The figure has no stated derivation in any of the four sources. It appears in the written draft (pages 4 and 5), on slide 5, in the one-pager's flow diagram, and in the transcript, always as a bare recommendation. The Colorado analysis findings cited for Recommendation 4 endorse continuing oversight qualitatively and give no number, and Memoru is cited for the two-case model rather than for hours. Every other figure in the document is derived; this one is not.

What the record adds is that the consultation period is where acuity rises. Answering Ian Dunn on July 17 you said trainees "start out With lower acuity conditions and then, you know, as they're doing their supervised consultation hours that they would, you know, be able to work on more complex cases," and, after his reply that he "just wanted to make sure that they would eventually deal with complex cases before they were fully licensed to practice by themselves," that "as soon as they're in the consultation phase that they've completed the lower acuity and that they've had the supervision, 10 hours and the group that I think they could certainly take on more difficult cases."

Since step 2 of the practicum is expressly limited to low-acuity cases, the consultation period is the only place in the design where a trainee meets complexity under supervision before full permitting.

Thirty is drafted, both for that reason and because a range in a rule enacts its floor. **Is 30 the number, or is 20 the floor with 30 as the target? And should the rule say anything about acuity during consultation, which none of the documents currently does?**

**5. May a practicum participant be another student in the program?**

Step 1 is described as a "retreat or peer-support model." The drafted definition at 7.35.3.29 B does not exclude trainees serving as each other's participants. **Intended, or should students be excluded?**

**7. Supervisory hours: inside the practicum total or on top of it?**

Your totals read as inside: 62 plus 10 equals 72. Drafted as inside, and stated expressly so the published rule's ambiguity does not survive. **Confirming.**

**8. Site supervisors: is there a minimum experience floor you would want?**

New 7.35.3.19 F(2) sets one year in the applicable certification. That figure appears nowhere in your documents or in either transcript. It is the most obviously adjustable number in the redline. **One year, higher, or competency-based instead?**

**9. End-of-life: your table says permittees who will see end-of-life participants "are required to have end-of-life practicum experience." Is that a practicum requirement, the consultation checkpoint, or both?**

Drafted as one provision, 7.35.3.19 J, covering both, conditioning unsupervised end-of-life practice rather than certification. **Should it instead be a condition of certification for everyone?**

**10. PTSD: hours, or the collaboration rule, or both?**

PTSD was requested on the record as a standalone curriculum topic, alongside substance use disorder, end of life, and suicidality. Your trauma module is explicitly "not PTSD treatment training," and your alternative is a scope rule: "if PTSD treatment is outside the Licensed Provider's scope of practice, collaboration with a PTSD-trained provider is recommended or required" (page 2).

PTSD hours are not drafted, on the reasoning that a module adequate to your description would not fit inside 84 hours. The collaboration rule is not drafted either, because it belongs in 7.35.3.13 B, scope of work, which is outside the scope handed to this drafting. **Do you want the collaboration rule pursued separately, and is "recommended" or "required" the right setting?**
