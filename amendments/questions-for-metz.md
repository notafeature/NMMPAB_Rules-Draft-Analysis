# Questions for Dr. Metz

Each of these changes drafting. They are ordered by how much turns on the answer. Questions 1 to 4 change numbers or structure in the redline. Questions 5 to 9 close gaps where the drafting had to pick something and the recommendations do not say.

Sources read in full before writing these: the six-page written draft of July 17, 2026, the one-page summary of the same date, and the six-slide committee deck of the same date. All three are byte-identical to the copies supplied July 25.

---

**1. Step 1 with well participants: were you assuming the statute already permits it, or assuming a change?**

Recommendation 3 step 1 is "Initial facilitation experience with well participants (approximately 30 hours). Two to three sessions as a facilitator in a retreat or peer-support model" (written draft, page 3; slide 4 states it the same way and adds "Prep and integration for every participant").

Nothing in 7.35.3 NMAC authorizes administering psilocybin to a person who is not a qualified patient. 7.35.3.14, page 9, authorizes provision only "to qualified patients." The July 17 morning board transcript treats this as a legislative question: "impossible to do adequate training without having the ability for people to use psilocybin in the training," said while hoping "that there's going to be a fix-it bill."

It is drafted as new section 7.35.3.29 with an express contingency, and 7.35.3.19 D(1) has a fallback limb, so the practicum works either way. **Do you read the Medical Psilocybin Act as already permitting this, or was step 1 written on the assumption of a statutory change?** If the former, the contingency clause comes out and the section becomes operative on day one.

---

**2. Is the 84 inclusive of the 5 simulated-patient hours, or on top of them?**

The curriculum table has no simulated-patient row, and the published rule has a 5-hour simulated patient experience at 7.35.3.18 C. It is drafted as 84 didactic plus 5 simulated, so 89 module hours. **Is that what you intended, or is the 5 meant to sit inside the 84?**

---

**3. Practicum totals: your 62 and 72 assume roughly 30 hours with well participants. If that step is not available at launch, which way do you want it to go?**

Two options, and the arithmetic differs:

- **(a)** Keep roughly 62 and 72 and let stage one be observation with qualified patients. Program totals become about 176 and 186 with 30 consultation hours, still above the published 150 and 170.
- **(b)** Raise the practicum so the qualified-patient contact is not thinned. This is what is drafted: 80 and 90, with program totals of 199 and 209.

The drafting picked (b) so that the total rises on every reading of the published rule, including the reading that puts the published practitioner total at 190. **Do you prefer (a)?**

---

**4. Consultation: 20 or 30?**

The recommendation says "20-30 total hours." A range stated in a rule operates as its floor, so drafting the range would enact 20. It is drafted at 30. **Is 30 the number you want, or was 20 the real floor with 30 as aspiration?**

---

**5. May a practicum participant be another student in the program?**

Step 1 is described as a "retreat or peer-support model." The drafted definition of practicum participant at 7.35.3.29 B does not exclude trainees serving as each other's participants. **Did you intend that, or should students be excluded?**

---

**6. Ethics at roughly 20 percent: 20 percent of what?**

The recommendation ties ethics to "roughly 20% of didactic hours" (written draft, page 2). Twenty percent of 84 is about 17. Twenty percent of the 70-hour core module, which is where the topic list lives, is 14. It is drafted at 14 inside the core module. **Which denominator did you mean?**

---

**7. Supervisory hours: inside the practicum total or on top of it?**

Your totals read as inside: 62 plus 10 equals 72. The published rule has the same ambiguity in the other direction, since 7.35.3.19 C says "an additional minimum of 20 hours" without saying additional to what. It is drafted as inside, and stated expressly so the ambiguity does not survive. **Confirming that is what you meant.**

---

**8. Site supervisors: is there a minimum experience floor you would want?**

The recommendation raises the vetting question and leaves it to the committee (written draft, page 6; slide 6). New 7.35.3.19 F(2) sets one year in the applicable certification. That figure appears nowhere in your documents or in either transcript. It is the most obviously adjustable number in the redline. **Is one year right, or higher, or should it be competency-based instead?**

---

**9. End-of-life: your table says permittees who will see end-of-life participants "are required to have end-of-life practicum experience." Is that a practicum requirement, the consultation checkpoint, or both?**

Recommendation 4 separately requires "at least one co-facilitated end-of-life case and present at least one end-of-life case in supervision" (written draft, page 5; slide 5). It is drafted as one provision, new 7.35.3.19 J, covering both, and it conditions unsupervised end-of-life practice rather than certification. **Should it instead be a condition of certification for everyone?**

---

**10. PTSD: hours, or the collaboration rule, or both?**

PTSD was requested on the record as a standalone curriculum topic at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting, alongside substance use disorder, end of life, and suicidality. Your trauma module is explicitly "not PTSD treatment training," and your alternative is a scope rule: "if PTSD treatment is outside the Licensed Provider's scope of practice, collaboration with a PTSD-trained provider is recommended or required" (written draft, page 2).

PTSD hours are not drafted, on the reasoning that a module adequate to your description would not fit inside 84 hours. The collaboration rule is not drafted either, because it belongs in 7.35.3.13 B, scope of work, which is outside the scope handed to this drafting. **Do you want the collaboration rule pursued separately, and is "recommended" or "required" the right setting?**
