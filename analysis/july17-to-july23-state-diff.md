# The rule as of July 17, against the rule published July 23

## Why this is a state diff and not a document diff

There is no July 17 version of the rule. The rule text in circulation on July 17 was the **July 9 draft**. This is established on the record, not inferred: during the afternoon committee meeting a member of the public asked about the reciprocity dates in "the document that was sent out to the public" and described "two different dates ... One is the end of 2026 and one is June 2027." Those are the July 9 dates. The department's answer was that "those dates were placeholders."

The only new rule language produced on July 17 was the revised in-person examination provision, which the department read into the record and pasted into the meeting chat during the morning board session.

So the meaningful comparison is not between two PDFs. It is between **the state of the rule at the end of July 17**, meaning the July 9 text as modified by every decision, announcement, and commitment made that day, and **the text published on July 23**.

## 1. Practicum hours: deferred, then published unchanged

**July 17, morning.** The board voted unanimously to send the practicum hours back to committee. Ian Dunn, board chair, presiding:

> "we can move to a vote on to defer the training and education practicum and didactic hours to the training and education committee for them to solve to reassess and re-come together and have time to come up with those updated standards"

> "I see seven hands ... No, Casper, the motion is adopted. We will send that back to the Training and Education Committee."

**State at end of July 17:** open. No number settled. Assigned to the committee.

**July 23, 7.35.3.19 (A) and (C), pp. 12 and 13:**

> "The practicum shall consist of a minimum of 100 hours of supervised practice training for facilitators and 120 hours for practitioners"

> "Practitioners shall complete an additional minimum of 20 hours as a practitioner supervising facilitators during in-person administration day sessions"

Identical to the July 9 text. The deferral produced no change.

## 2. Didactic hours: deferred, then increased

The same motion covered didactic hours. They did not stay the same. They went up.

**July 9, and therefore the text in circulation on July 17:**

> "All practitioners and facilitators shall complete a psilocybin therapy module consisting of a minimum of 25 didactic hours, prior to applying for certification"

with, in the same list:

> "A simulated patient experience of no less than 5 hours in addition to the 25 didactic hours"

**July 23, 7.35.3.18 (C), p. 11:**

> "All practitioners and facilitators shall complete a psilocybin therapy module consisting of a minimum of 30 didactic hours and 5 hours of simulated patient experience, prior to applying for certification"

A five-hour increase, published six days after the board voted to send this number to committee. The required topic list also grew from 14 items to 17. The three additions:

- Trauma-Informed Care
- Dosing
- Non-ordinary states of consciousness

**Note in fairness:** adding Trauma-Informed Care is responsive to what was presented on July 17. Dr. Anne Metz recommended trauma as a standalone content area rather than material nested inside other modules. The department moved toward the committee on curriculum content in the same edit that moved away from it on hours.

## 3. In-person examination: carried through

**July 17, morning.** The department read the revised language into the record, describing three pathways. Dominic Zerlo, director, Center for Medical Cannabis and Psilocybin:

> "the certifying clinician would still need to conduct an in-person exam of the patient within the previous six months ... They could do that final appointment as telehealth if they have seen that patient in person in the previous six months. In addition to that, they could also do telehealth or telemedicine if they have done a review of the medical records pertaining to the diagnosis for the qualifying condition from a clinician who has examined the individual in person within the previous six months. Or if that is burdensome, they can do the same thing, but make sure that they have a formal consult with a clinician who has conducted an in-person exam in the previous six months."

**July 23, 7.35.3.8 (B)(8)(c), pp. 1 and 2.** All three pathways appear:

> "(i) the certifying clinician has conducted an in-person examination of the patient within the previous six months; (ii) the certifying clinician has conducted a patient evaluation via telemedicine and has reviewed the medical records pertaining to the diagnosis of a qualifying condition and has confirmed the medical records of the diagnosis are from a clinician who has conducted an in -person examination of the patient within the previous six months; or (iii) the certifying clinician has conducted a patient evaluation via telemedicine and has had a formal consult with a clinician who has conducted an in-person exam with the patient within the previous six months"

Carried through as described.

## 6. The training permit, and the authorization that is not there

This is the item where the July 17 record and the published text diverge most consequentially, because the reason given on July 17 for not needing a change is not supported by the text that was published.

**July 17, afternoon.** Dr. Metz recommended a time-limited training permit, on the reasoning that it "gives trainees a lawful pathway to get their required client contact." The department's response, Dominic Zerlo, responding to a point raised by Denali Wilson:

> "that's already was part of the idea as far as not needing another permit with that practicum because those students already will have that kind of protection ability to be there as a part of being registered as a student with an educational program"

**State at end of July 17:** a training permit was described as unnecessary, because registered students were said to already have the necessary protection.

**July 23.** The rule requires students to handle the work:

7.35.3.19 (A), pp. 12 and 13, requires students to be "provided the opportunity to experience, observe, or conduct supervised facilitation of administration day sessions in-person."

7.34.3.20 (H)(5), p. 14, counts students toward mandatory staffing, "a minimum of one facilitator or qualified student for every two patients," with a "qualified student" defined as one "registered with a certified educational program" who has "completed at least 50 hours of their practicum."

But the provision that grants authority to possess and administer psilocybin, 7.34.3.14, p. 9, does not mention students:

> "Certification of a practitioner, facilitator, or healing center shall enable practitioners, facilitators, and owners and employees of healing centers to do the following"

The subsections that follow grant possession and administration authority to practitioners, to facilitators, and to "owners and employees of healing centers who are registered with the department." A practicum student is none of these. Students hold no certification, which is what the practicum is a prerequisite for.

**Stated precisely, because this is a legal question and not a drafting preference:** the published rule nowhere authorizes a practicum student to possess or administer medical psilocybin, and 7.34.3.14 is the provision that performs that function for every other category of person in the room. A student who is separately employed by a healing center and registered with the department might be covered through that route, but that is a property of some students rather than a rule about students, and the practicum requirement does not depend on it.

The consequence: Recommendation 3's training permit is not a preference about program design. It is the mechanism that supplies an authorization the published rule assumes and does not grant.

## 7. Students in the staffing ratio: taken back, and delivered

**July 17, afternoon.** Zerlo, on the same point raised by Denali Wilson:

> "you brought up an interesting part about them, the students at that point counting towards part of the ratio. And we can definitely take a look at that if that would be after they've completed so many hours to be a part of that rather than needing to create an entire other permit or certification level. But we could definitely look at that. I'll take that on back as well."

**July 23.** Delivered. 7.34.3.20 (H)(5) is new; the phrases "qualified student," "for every two patients," and "every eight patients" appear zero times in the July 9 draft. The threshold is 50 practicum hours, which matches the "after they've completed so many hours" framing.

So the department implemented this suggestion inside six days. It did so without the authorization described in item 6, which is what makes item 6 a defect rather than an omission.

## 8, 9, 10. Published but never discussed at either July 17 meeting

Neither transcript contains any discussion of the following, each of which changes what a person must do:

**8. A new practicum entry gate.** 7.35.3.19 (A), p. 12, now bars starting the practicum until the student has completed "at least half of the didactic requirements and all of the simulated patient requirements." No equivalent appears in the July 9 text.

**9. Waiver session counts tightened from "or" to "and".** The July 9 waiver asked for "two individual sessions or two group sessions." The July 23 rule requires both, and the two waivers do not agree with each other: 7.35.3.10 (D)(1) requires two individual and two group sessions, while 7.35.3.19 (G)(4) requires two individual and one group session.

**10. Reciprocity supervisory hours deleted.** The requirement that reciprocity practitioners complete the 20 supervisory practicum hours in New Mexico is gone from 7.35.3.10.

## 11. Curriculum requests made on the record, and not reflected

**July 17, afternoon.** Denali Wilson asked for indication-specific didactic requirements, in the context of who may serve uniquely vulnerable populations:

> "if we cannot create a specific version, certification requirement for people to provide care to vulnerable, specifically uniquely vulnerable populations ... then I think it's that much more important that we have specific didactic requirements for those indications. So I think from what's been presented, you know, that would be substance use disorder, end of life, suicidality, PTSD, that that be incorporated into curriculum requirements."

Chris Caldwell, End of Life Psychedelic Care, argued for a substantial end-of-life module. Larry Leeman supported roughly 10 to 15 hours of end-of-life content in the core curriculum.

**July 23.** The 17-item curriculum list in 7.35.3.18 (C) contains no end-of-life content, no suicidality content, and no substance use disorder content. Trauma-Informed Care was added; the others were not.

This is notable given that nicotine use disorder is an approved indication, and that the July 17 morning session discussed end-of-life access at length.

## What this diff establishes

1. The two numbers the board voted 7-0 to send back to committee were published without committee input. One of them was published five hours higher than it stood on the day of the vote.
2. Three requirements that change what a person must do were published without being raised at either July 17 meeting.
3. One requirement, the training permit, was declined on a stated basis that the published text does not support, while the published text simultaneously requires students to do work it does not authorize them to do.
4. The department carried through faithfully on the in-person exam, the CS number, the reciprocity dates, and the staffing ratio, all within six days.

Point 4 is not a concession. It is what makes points 1 through 3 legible as something other than a scheduling accident.
