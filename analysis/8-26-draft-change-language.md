# Proposed change language for the August 25 texts

Drafting material for public comment on the revised proposed rule 7.35.3 NMAC and the proposed amendments to 7.35.2 NMAC, both published August 25, 2026. Written comment is received until the close of the rule hearing, October 2, 2026, and the department has said on the record that changes can still be made through the hearing, as they were in the producer rulemaking.

This file is drafting material, not site content and not a filing. Nothing in it has been submitted to the department. It states proposed edits in the published texts' own strike-and-add convention: struck text in [brackets], inserted text in **bold**. Where a proposal is the committee recommendation's position it is attributed to the recommendation; where it is a community position it is stated as one. The formal redline documents in `amendments/` and `amendments-remainder/` cover the July 23 text; this file drafts against August 25 and does not modify either of those documents. The controlled-substance number, which `amendments-remainder/` excludes by decision, is addressed here in Part 4 as its own track.

---

## Part 1. The role architecture

The published texts carry three certified individual roles with names that do not describe the work: the certifying clinician, who the department itself described on August 21 as ensuring medical clearance; the practitioner, a name that collides with "nurse practitioner" and says nothing about the role; and the facilitator. The recommendation's architecture names each role by its function and uses "practitioner" for what it is in ordinary language: anyone who practices in the program.

### 1.1 "Practitioner" becomes the umbrella term

In 7.35.2.7 NMAC, subsection P, replace the practitioner definition:

> "Practitioner" means [a clinician who is certified by the department to provide medical psilocybin integrative therapy, supervise facilitators, and who has completed department required trainings] **any individual certified by the department to provide services to qualified patients in the medical psilocybin program. A medical screener is a practitioner. A licensed provider is a practitioner. A facilitator is a practitioner. Each provides a service the program defines.**

### 1.2 The middle role becomes the licensed provider

Insert in 7.35.2.7, subsection L:

> **"Licensed provider" means a clinician who holds a current license from a recognized New Mexico licensing board allowing the delivery of medical, counseling, mental health, or behavioral health care, and who is certified by the department to provide medical psilocybin integrative therapy and to supervise facilitators, having completed department-required trainings.**

The remainder is conforming amendment: every occurrence of "practitioner" in the role sense throughout 7.35.3 NMAC and the amended 7.35.2 becomes "licensed provider." The August 25 text uses the word 72 times; the substitution is mechanical, with each instance checked against the new umbrella sense.

### 1.3 The screening role becomes the medical screener, and screening is what it does

Replace the certifying clinician definition in 7.35.2.7, subsection C:

> "[Certifying clinician] **Medical screener**" means a clinician [who holds a state of New Mexico controlled substances number, who is certified by the department to diagnose or confirm a previous diagnosis of a qualifying condition and to evaluate the medical appropriateness of a patient enrolling in the New Mexico psilocybin program] **certified by the department to conduct medical screening of an applicant for patient enrollment: to verify that a qualifying condition has been attested in the application, to assess the applicant's medical readiness, and to identify contraindications to the medical use of psilocybin. The medical screener does not diagnose the qualifying condition and is not required to review the clinical records of the diagnosis.**

The drafting rationale, stated for the comment record:

- **The department's own description of the role supports it.** At the August 21 committee meeting, the department described the certifying clinician as "ensuring the medical clearance," with the counseling-side judgment sitting with the treating role. The published definition instead authorizes diagnosis. The definition should say what the department says the role does.
- **The Act permits it.** The Act's two-part test requires a clinician's judgment of medical appropriateness "based on being diagnosed with a qualifying condition." Nothing in the Act requires the judging clinician to be the diagnostician or to hold the diagnostic records; the diagnosis can reach the screener as an attestation. The application structure already works this way: 7.35.3.8 (B)(8)(a) has the applicant attest that "The applicant has a qualifying diagnosis."
- **The confidentiality exposure shrinks.** The published pathway at 7.35.3.8 (B)(8)(c)(ii) requires the screener to review "the medical records pertaining to the diagnosis of a qualifying condition." For substance use disorder, one of the four qualifying conditions, those records carry federal protection under 42 CFR Part 2. An attestation-based screening confirms that a protected diagnosis exists without moving the protected records to a clinician who will not treat the patient. Conforming edit at 7.35.3.8 (B)(8)(c)(ii):

> the certifying clinician has conducted a patient evaluation via telemedicine and has [reviewed the medical records pertaining to the diagnosis of a qualifying condition and has confirmed the medical records of the diagnosis are from] **received a written attestation of the diagnosis of a qualifying condition from** a clinician who has conducted an in-person examination of the patient within the previous six months; or

Conforming amendments follow through 7.35.3: the term change everywhere the role appears (54 occurrences), and the "qualified patient" definition in 7.35.2.7, which currently reads "whose certifying clinician has judged the patient to be a medically appropriate candidate."

### 1.4 The clinician umbrella, and the Act's own word restored

The published text already builds the hierarchy this part names. Both clinical roles are defined as subtypes of clinician: the certifying clinician "means a clinician who holds a state of New Mexico controlled substances number," and the practitioner means "a clinician who is certified by the department to provide medical psilocybin integrative therapy," with supervision of facilitators and the department trainings completing that definition (amendments PDF p. 3). The facilitator is defined as "an individual," not a clinician. The clinical treatment authority therefore already sits with the middle role. The edits below state the hierarchy the text implies, and they let the screening role's clinical involvement shrink to screening without creating any authority that does not already exist.

In 7.35.2.7, the clinician definition:

> "Clinician" means an approved health care provider licensed in New Mexico who holds a certification from the department to provide [medical services] **medical psilocybin services** to qualified patients. **The [certifying clinician] medical screener and the [practitioner] licensed provider are clinicians. A facilitator is not required to be a clinician.**

If the renames in 1.2 and 1.3 are not taken, the published names substitute in the inserted sentences. The one-word aim fix in the first sentence is a correction in its own right, stated at 3.11: the published clinician definition reaches only "medical services," the three sessions, which the certifying clinician does not provide.

In 7.35.2.7, the qualified patient definition:

> "Qualified patient" or "patient" means a patient whose [certifying clinician] **clinician** has judged the patient to be a medically appropriate candidate for the use of medical psilocybin based on being diagnosed with a qualifying condition.

The Medical Psilocybin Act defines a qualified patient as one "whose clinician has judged the patient to be a medically appropriate candidate for the use of medical psilocybin based on being diagnosed with a qualifying condition"; the amendment substitutes the narrower defined term, certifying clinician. Restoring the Act's word conforms the definition to the statute, and it returns the statutory judgment of medical appropriateness to the clinician umbrella rather than fixing it, by definition, in the one role that carries the controlled-substance number. Part 4 states what follows for the number.

### 1.5 What this part does not resolve

The controlled-substance number sits inside the published definition, so any redefinition confronts it. Part 4 states the options. A screening-only definition weakens the stated rationale for a prescriber credential, and the two edits can be proposed together or separately.

## Part 2. The hours and the competency checkpoint

### 2.1 Per-area minimums, or floors in guidance

The recommendation's position: 80 didactic hours with minimums in nine content areas summing to 63. The published 7.35.3.18 (C)(1) lists 26 topics with no hours attached to any of them. Two forms of the edit, both on the record from August 21:

**The recommendation's form.** Attach the minimums to the list: core psychotherapy skills and ethics, 22 hours; end-of-life and palliative care considerations, 8; trauma and trauma-informed care, 7; preparation and integration sessions, 6; medicine, dosing, and clinical research, 5; screening, suicidality, and crisis response, 5; administration session, 4; management of challenging psychedelic experiences, 4; role playing and/or simulated patient experience, 2.

**The middle course.** The rule names the areas; the numbers live in guidance the rule points to, so the department keeps objective review criteria and adjusting a number does not require a rule hearing. Insert at the close of 7.35.3.18 (C)(1):

> **The department shall establish, in guidance documents, minimum instructional hours for the topic areas of this paragraph, and shall evaluate each educational program's curriculum against those minimums in its review under 7.35.3.15.**

This answers the department's two stated objections: reciprocity crosswalks show topics rather than hours, and per-condition rigidity would force rule modifications. Guidance floors do neither.

**Regardless of either form**, two topics should be named in the list, because they are the clinical core of three of the four qualifying conditions and appear nowhere in it: **end-of-life and palliative care**, and **suicidality**. The nearest published topic is "Education on the qualifying conditions and appropriate treatment practices."

### 2.2 Simulated patient hours

The published requirement is 10 hours, split five in person and five flexible, at 7.35.3.18 (C)(2). The recommendation set 2 inside its 80. On August 21, after the department's walkthrough, the committee's hours author supported approximately six, three of exercises and practice and three of simulation, to preserve room for electives. The edit: in (C)(2), [no less than five hours] twice becomes **no less than three hours**, or the paragraph is restated at a six-hour total. The same paragraph needs the drafting correction in Part 3.6 either way.

### 2.3 The consultation group

The published 7.35.3.19 (C) takes the recommendation's two-case checkpoint and removes its structure: presentations go to the practicum supervisor alone, with no consultation group and no dedicated hours. The recommendation's rationale is independence: a supervisor grading their own program's student is not external gatekeeping, and the consultation group is the answer to the pay-to-play critique of psychedelic training. The department and the committee's hours author agreed on the record, August 21, that a combination of practice group and supervisor is better than the supervisor alone. The edit:

> C. Practicum evaluation: As part of the minimum required practicum hours, the student shall present a minimum of two of the last four patients [to the practicum supervisor] as case presentations for evaluation **within at least 18 hours of case presentation and consultation with educational program faculty, which the program may provide through a contracted third party**, and must receive a passing grade on the case presentations to complete their practicum. **The consultation hours remain valid for up to 12 months after the practicum is completed.**

### 2.4 The staging, named

The published practicum carries the recommendation's direction without its names: the first 20 administration-day hours are with low-risk patients. If the totals of 100 and 120 stand, the sequencing should still be stated as stages so a student and a program can plan against it. This is the weakest edit in this file as drafting, and the recommendation's full staged structure, 102 and 114 hours beginning with well participants, is blocked on the department's stated reading that the statute does not allow practicum work with individuals who do not have a qualifying condition. That reading, if contested, is contested at the statute, not in this rule.

## Part 3. Drafting corrections

Corrections the text needs regardless of any position, each with the language that resolves it. All are carried in fuller form in `analysis/8-25-published-delta.md` part 6 and the site's provision notes.

**3.1 The two 40-hour waivers disagree.** 7.35.3.10 (D)(1) requires "two separate individual sessions and two separate group sessions"; 7.35.3.19 (H)(4) requires two individual sessions and "a minimum of one group session" for the same 40 contact hours. Both cannot be the standard. Either conforming edit resolves it; the choice is the department's to make explicitly rather than by leaving both.

**3.2 No provision authorizes a student to possess or administer psilocybin.** 7.35.3.19 (A) requires students to conduct supervised facilitation of administration sessions and 7.35.3.20 (H)(5) counts students toward staffing, while 7.35.3.14 grants handling authority to three categories, none of them a student. Insert in 7.35.3.14:

> **(E) Students: An individual registered as a student with a certified educational program who has completed the simulated patient requirements of 7.35.3.18 may possess and administer medical psilocybin to qualified patients as part of practicum training under the direct supervision of a certified [practitioner] licensed provider, at an approved healing center or other approved location.**

**3.3 The registration 7.35.3.14 (C) requires does not exist.** Subsection (C) conditions healing-center owner and employee authority on being "registered with the department," and no section creates that registration. The August 25 text supplies the hook: applications now list all employees by legal name, 7.35.3.11 (A)(22) and (B). The edit: in 7.35.3.14 (C), [who are registered with the department] becomes **who are listed in the healing center's application or its required updates under 7.35.3.11**.

**3.4 The New Mexico module has no date by which it must exist.** Every certification pathway requires it; nothing obliges the department to produce it. Insert at 7.35.3.18 (A): **The department shall create or approve the New Mexico module no later than [a date ahead of the program's first certifications].**

**3.5 The evaluator conflict rule disqualifies every evaluator the rule requires.** 7.35.3.16 requires programs to engage and pay a third-party evaluation team, and 7.35.3.16 (C)(2)(b) disqualifies anyone "receiving compensation from" the program. Insert in (C)(2)(b): **Compensation for conducting the evaluation itself does not constitute a conflict under this subparagraph.**

**3.6 A doubled sentence and stray punctuation.** 7.35.3.18 (C)(2) reads "five hours,;" and states its competency-evaluation sentence twice. Strike the duplicate sentence and the stray comma.

**3.7 A heading misprint.** The 7.35.3.25 heading prints "7.34.3.25." Its own history note reads 7.35.3.25.

**3.8 One term defined twice.** The amended 7.35.2.7 defines "Producer" at subsection P, item (9), and "Psilocybin producer' or 'producer'" at item (15), with near-identical text. One should be struck.

**3.9 The amendment's header does not match its body.** The amendments document opens "This is an amendment to 7.35.2 NMAC, Sections 7, 10, and 22"; its body and the hearing notice amend sections 7, 10, and 24.

**3.10 List punctuation in the practicum criteria.** 7.35.3.19 (A) places "; and" after item (2) although items (3) and (4) follow, and item (3) ends in a period rather than a semicolon.

**3.11 The clinician definition does not cover the certifying clinician.** "Clinician" reaches a provider certified to provide "medical services"; "medical services" means services "before, during and after the ingestion of psilocybin and includes a preparation session, an administration session and an integration session"; and the certifying clinician, defined as a subtype of clinician, provides none of the three. The one-word correction: in the clinician definition, [medical services] becomes **medical psilocybin services**, the amendments' own broader term. That broader term's definition is itself missing a word, reading "any medical or therapeutic service related the medical use of psilocybin," and wants "related **to** the medical use."

## Part 4. The controlled-substance number

Not a recommendation item; a community position with its own record since June. The requirement now lives in three places, so the full edit is three strikes:

- In the amended 7.35.2.7 definition of certifying clinician: strike **"who holds a state of New Mexico controlled substances number,"**
- Strike 7.35.3.9 (D)(2), "NM controlled substance number," from the application packet, renumbering the list.
- In 7.35.3.8 (B)(3), strike the number from the patient application's record of the clinician's licensure.

The middle positions on the record, if the full strike is not taken: the consultation model (July 9, board member Chris Peskuski) and the behavioral-health referral with telemedicine (July 9, Keenan Ryan). The screening-only definition in Part 1.3 is the quiet version of the same move: a role confined to medical screening weakens the rationale for a prescriber credential. The restoration of the Act's word clinician in the qualified-patient definition, Part 1.4, is another: it returns the statutory judgment of medical appropriateness to the clinician umbrella, so the judgment is no longer fixed by definition in the number-holding role even if the number stays. The analysis of what the number is, who can hold it, and who it excludes is on the site's controlled-substance number page.

## Part 5. The owner statement at the patient's residence

Not a recommendation item; a community position resting on a committee member's written comment.

**What the text requires.** 7.35.3.11 (B) certifies "other approved locations," which "may include a patient's residence," for a patient who cannot be physically transported to a healing center, for other reasons of medical necessity, for rural and frontier access, or for treatment in a natural environment. Item (B)(9) reads in full: "Proof of ownership by the qualified patient or attending practitioner or facilitator of any property on which medical psilocybin administration sessions will be conducted, or a signed, written statement from the owner of such property acknowledging that the owner understands that persons will be participating in the medical psilocybin program on the premises, and what those persons are authorized to do within the terms of their certification" (NEW p. 6). The healing-center application carries the same instrument at (A)(8) for a commercial premises; this part addresses only (B)(9).

**What it does.** A patient who rents cannot receive treatment at home unless the landlord signs a statement acknowledging that the household is participating in the medical psilocybin program. The statement discloses the patient's program participation, and with it the existence of a qualifying condition, to a private party with no role in the patient's care, as a condition of receiving care. It also hands that party an effective veto over the treatment. The same application requires, two items earlier at (B)(7), a plan for maintaining patient confidentiality at the location whose owner the application has just informed. The at-home pathway exists in the first instance for patients who cannot travel, end-of-life patients among them.

**The record.** Denali Wilson, in her July 25, 2026 comments on the committee's redline of the then-published text: "seems like this will require EOL patients who are getting at home treatment to get permission from their landlord. We don't require that for any other in home healthcare and it's not appropriate to require that for EOL patients. People should not have to get their landlords permission to have certain healthcare." The comment file carries no section anchors; the content fixes it to this provision. The August 25 text carries the requirement forward.

**The resolving language.** Ownership stays as one form of proof; lawful occupancy becomes another; the owner acknowledgment survives only for a location that belongs to none of the participants, and no instrument names the patient:

> (9) Proof of ownership by the qualified patient or attending practitioner or facilitator of any property on which medical psilocybin administration sessions will be conducted, **or documentation of the qualified patient's lawful occupancy of a residence, such as a lease or rental agreement**[, or a signed, written statement from the owner of such property acknowledging that the owner understands that persons will be participating in the medical psilocybin program on the premises, and what those persons are authorized to do within the terms of their certification]**. Where the location is neither owned nor occupied by the qualified patient, the attending practitioner, or the facilitator, the application shall include a signed, written statement from the owner of the property consenting to its temporary use. Documentation or a statement submitted under this paragraph shall not identify any person as a program participant or disclose any patient's enrollment in the medical psilocybin program.**
