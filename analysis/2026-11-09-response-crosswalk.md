# The November 9 response to comments against the written comments and the hearing

The department's office of general counsel answered the public comments on the proposed 7.35.3 NMAC on November 9, 2026, in eleven topics, without naming any commenter or counting the comments. This file sets that response beside the 75 written comments in the final exhibit binder and the 28 people who spoke at the October 2 rule hearing, so that each of the department's positions can be traced to the comments that raised it, and each request the response does not answer can be found. Everything the response proposes is proposed, not adopted; the text in force on this site remains the August 25 publication.

## The property-owner statement

The text heard October 2 required, for a healing center, "Proof of ownership of any property on which medical psilocybin administration sessions will be conducted, or a signed, written statement from the owner of such property acknowledging that the owner understands that persons will be participating in the medical psilocybin program on the premises" (7.35.3.11 (A)(8), p. 5), and the same for an other approved location, with ownership "by the qualified patient or attending practitioner or facilitator" (7.35.3.11 (B)(9), p. 6). An other approved location may be a patient's home, so a patient who rents could be treated at home only with the owner's signed statement.

54 of the 75 written comments asked for the statement to be deleted or narrowed, or for an exemption from it. 13 of the 28 hearing speakers raised it. The written comments came from patients, clinicians, and these organizations and officials, among others:

- 8.GG, End of Life Options New Mexico
- 8.MMM, Disability Rights New Mexico
- 8.Y, the Psychedelic Bar Association's New Mexico Working Group
- 8.D, the Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies
- 8.C, Healing Advocacy, the Psychedelic Mental Health Access Alliance, and Rudick Law Group
- 8.A, Ian Dunn, who signs as chair of the Medical Psilocybin Advisory Board
- 8.JJJ, Larry Leeman, a member of the Advisory Board
- 8.FFF, a memorandum over the name of Martin Hickey, MD, prepared by Anne L. Metz

At the hearing, Senator Jeff Steinborn, who introduced himself as the Act's primary author, said, "I am totally opposed to the landlord sign-off for homebound patients" (1:33:12), and opposed the same requirement for siting a healing center (1:34:20). Brett Phelps, for the Psychedelic Bar Association's working group, asked that "the department exempt a patient's own residence from this requirement" (1:28:04). Larry Leeman said the End of Life Care Committee "felt really strongly about the bill landlord, uh, provision needing to be struck" (1:01:49). Denali Wilson, for Healing Advocacy, asked the hearing officer by name for "special attention" to it (1:45:19).

Many of the written comments share sentences word for word. Of the 75:

- 28 contain "Delete paragraph (B)(9) of 7.35.3.11" (marked D in the index below)
- 19 contain "This provision shall not apply to end-of-life patients or others seeking in-home care, or to long-term care facility residents" (marked E in the index below)
- 13 contain "the patient documents their own legal right to occupy the residence, no owner signs anything" (marked C in the index below)
- 17 contain "Giving landlords the power to block care for vulnerable and disabled patients is contrary to that choice" (marked G in the index below)
- 38 contain "Remove the certifying clinician design" (marked R in the index below)
- 37 contain "Strike the patient application process" (marked S in the index below)

42 of the 75 contain at least one of these six sentences; most add the sender's own reasons around them. The exemption sentence (E) is the language End of Life Options New Mexico proposed in its letter at 8.GG, which 8.C and Denali Wilson's hearing comment (1:44:25) endorse by name.

The response states: "The Department received considerable public comment regarding the proposed requirements at sections 11(A) and (B) ... In consideration of the comments received, the Department intends to strike the landlord/property-owner statement requirements" (Response, p. 4). In place of the owner's statement, the applicant would attest "that they are the owner of the property or that they are aware of the legal risk concerning the property," and section 8 would require the patient to attest to having been informed of the legal status of psilocybin. The response treats the federally assisted housing concern as "effectively resolved by the removal of the landlord-approval requirement" (p. 4). The change is proposed; it takes effect only in an adopted rule.

## The certifying clinician

60 of the 75 written comments addressed the certifying clinician, and 11 of the 28 hearing speakers. One written comment supported the role as proposed: Henry T. Harbin, MD, "I think the guidelines for the Certifying clinician are appropriate" (8.CCC). The rest asked to remove it, to remove the controlled-substance registration from it (7.35.3.9 (D)(2), p. 3), or to replace a universal clearance with screening by the treating provider and referral where a risk is found.

The response proposes the screening-and-referral design. It grounds the change in the statute's definition of qualified patient, Section 26-2D-3(H) NMSA 1978, and in the rule's own definition of practitioner as a clinician certified by the department, so that "practitioner certification therefore comports with the statutory requirement" (Response, p. 2). It does not address the proposed amendments to 7.35.2 NMAC, whose definitions at 7.35.2.7 carry the certifying clinician and the controlled-substance number as published August 25.

## What the proposed revisions would change on each pathway

The pathways page (`docs/7.35.3/pathways.html`, built from `tools/parts/7.35.3/sync-pathways.py`) states the August 25 text. Set against the response, each route changes as follows. This section is derived: each line follows from the response page cited, and none is adopted text.

**Patient.** A patient is certified by a practitioner after the department's screening tool, not by a separate certifying clinician, and needs no in-person examination unless referred. A patient who has not seen a medical provider in the previous 12 months is referred to one before certification (Response, pp. 2 to 3). Enrollment is decided within 10 business days instead of 30 calendar days (p. 1). The patient attests to having been informed of the legal status of psilocybin (p. 4). A patient who rents needs no statement from the owner to be treated at home (p. 4).

**Certifying clinician.** The route ends. Every step on the page, the diagnosing license, the eight-hour training at 7.35.3.18 (B), the New Mexico controlled-substance number, the two-year certification, and the continuing education at 7.35.3.18 (G), belongs to a role the response proposes to remove (pp. 2 to 3). The amended 7.35.2.7 definition of certifying clinician remains in the 7.35.2 rulemaking, which the response does not address.

**Practitioner.** The practitioner takes on certification of patients: completing the screening tool, referring, and attesting that the benefits likely outweigh the risks and that any identified risk has a written plan (pp. 2 to 3). Qualifying licenses are listed at 7.35.3.9 (E)(1): licensed therapists, professional clinical counselors, mental health counselors, alcohol and drug abuse counselors, independent, clinical, and master social workers, psychologists, psychiatrists, psychiatric nurse practitioners, and advanced practice registered nurses, with a letter from a licensing body for any other license (pp. 5 to 6). In an administration session the practitioner need only be in the building (p. 5). Training and practicum hours are unchanged (p. 6); "suicidality response" is added to the curriculum (p. 7).

**Facilitator.** Supervision by a practitioner stays (p. 6). An individual session needs one practitioner and one more practitioner or facilitator; a group needs one practitioner per nine patients and one more practitioner, facilitator, or qualified student per three (p. 5). Practicum hours are unchanged (p. 6).

**Reciprocity.** Both reduced-practicum provisions read one group session (p. 7). The open step on the page, certifying clinicians named as eligible for reciprocity while the packet lists only practitioner and facilitator items, falls away with the certifying clinician.

**Healing center and other approved location.** The owner's statement at 7.35.3.11 (A)(8) and (B)(9) is replaced by the applicant's attestation of ownership or of awareness of the legal risk (p. 4). The staffing ratios change as above. Treatment records are kept at least five years (p. 10). Unchanged: the 90-day certification of an other approved location, the list of reasons for one, two wilderness-certified staff at 7.35.3.20 (K), and the ownership bar at 7.35.3.23 (pp. 4, 6, 9). No fee is charged for permits, certificates, or applications (p. 8).
