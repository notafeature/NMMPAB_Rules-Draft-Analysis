## Part 2. Training and education: BLOCKING findings

### B1. Students are required to work in administration sessions but have no authority to handle the medicine

**Sections 7.35.3.14 (page 9), 7.35.3.19 A (pages 12 to 13), 7.35.3.20 D and H(5) (page 14). NEW.**

7.35.3.14 is titled and framed as the exclusive authorization provision:

> "7.34.3.14 AUTHORIZED POSSESSION, PURCHASE, OR SALE OF MEDICAL PSILOCYBIN BY PRACTITIONERS, FACILITATORS, HEALING CENTER OWNERS AND EMPLOYEES: Certification of a practitioner, facilitator, or healing center shall enable practitioners, facilitators, and owners and employees of healing centers to do the following, in accordance with medical psilocybin program rules:"

Students appear nowhere in 7.35.3.14. Meanwhile 7.35.3.19 A requires:

> "Students shall participate in a minimum of 80 hours of administration day sessions, where students are provided the opportunity to experience, observe, or conduct supervised facilitation of administration day sessions in-person with a minimum of 14 different patients over a minimum of eight different administration and same-day sessions"

and 7.35.3.20 H(5) counts students toward the required staffing:

> "in group administration sessions a minimum of one practitioner for every eight patients and a minimum of one facilitator or qualified student for every two patients. For purposes of the foregoing provision, a student shall be deemed qualified if they are registered with a certified educational program and they have completed at least 50 hours of their practicum."

**The concern.** A student who conducts supervised facilitation, or who is counted as one of the required session staff, will handle psilocybin product. 7.35.3.14 does not authorize that. 7.35.3.22 (page 15) states that program participation "does not relieve the qualified patient or certificant from criminal prosecution or civil penalties for ... Unlawful distribution, transfer, or use of psilocybin." A student is not a certificant. This affects every practitioner and facilitator candidate, every educational program that must place them, and every healing center that hosts them.

**Diff note.** 7.35.3.14 has no counterpart in the July 9 draft. It is entirely new text. 7.35.3.20 H(5)'s "qualified student" clause is also new.

**Transcript note.** At the July 17 afternoon committee meeting, a proposal for a "training permit" was presented and the department response recorded in the transcript was that a permit "really wouldn't be necessary with the model that we have ... that already would be covered as them being a registered student with an educational program where they have that ability to be a part of those training sessions, including to conduct them under supervision" and that "those students already will have that kind of protection ability to be there as a part of being registered as a student with an educational program." The published text does not contain a provision producing that result.

**Severity: BLOCKING.**

### B2. The New Mexico module gates every pathway and has no delivery date

**Section 7.35.3.18 A (page 11). CARRIED OVER from July 9.**

> "All certifying clinicians, practitioners, and facilitators who provide medical psilocybin services, including all such providers who are certified by the department on the basis of having completed an educational program from another jurisdiction, shall complete a New Mexico educational module created or approved by the department prior to applying for certification"

7.35.3.10 B(3) (page 4) repeats the requirement for out-of-jurisdiction applicants: "Documentation of completion of the New Mexico required educational module". 7.35.3.17 B (page 11) carves the New Mexico module out of the test-out option, so no one may bypass it.

**The concern.** No certifying clinician, practitioner, or facilitator can be certified until this module exists and is available. The rule imposes no deadline on the department to create or approve it, no requirement that it be published, and no minimum or maximum hour count (every other module in 7.35.3.18 carries an hour minimum). This gates the entire workforce, including the out-of-jurisdiction pathway that is the only route to the two certified faculty that 7.35.3.12 A(22) requires programs to employ by December 31, 2027.

**Transcript note.** The July 17 afternoon committee agenda as read into the record included the open question "Reciprocity, when will the New Mexico modules be available for reciprocity?" The transcript contains no answer to that question.

**Severity: BLOCKING.**

### B3. The third-party evaluator conflict rule disqualifies the evaluators the program must hire

**Section 7.35.3.16 A and C(2)(b) (page 10). CARRIED OVER from July 9.**

> "A. General requirements: A psilocybin educational program shall engage a qualified third-party evaluation team to conduct the evaluations required under this rule."

> "(b) Holding a financial interest in, receiving compensation from, or serving in any governance or advisory capacity with the program or organization being evaluated."

**The concern.** Subsection A obliges the program to engage the team. Engagement means the program pays the team. Subsection C(2)(b) makes "receiving compensation from ... the program or organization being evaluated" a disqualifying conflict, with no carve-out for the evaluation fee itself. Read as written, no person can lawfully serve. Since 7.35.3.12 A(20) makes the resulting report a required component of a complete application, this defect propagates to every educational program application.

**Severity: BLOCKING** as drafted. The fix is narrow: exclude the evaluation engagement fee from the definition of disqualifying compensation.

### B4. Practicum cannot be performed at launch, and the only relief is a standardless waiver

**Section 7.35.3.19 A and D (pages 12 to 13). Hours CARRIED OVER; the entry gate is NEW.**

> "The practicum shall consist of a minimum of 100 hours of supervised practice training for facilitators and 120 hours for practitioners and shall be completed prior to applying for certification."

> "D. Practicum location: All practicum hours shall take place in an approved healing center or other approved location."

**The concern, stated as a chain.** Each student needs 14 different qualified patients across at least eight administration day sessions, including two group sessions with four or more patients each. Those hours must occur at an approved healing center or other approved location. Other approved locations are available only in the narrow circumstances set out in 7.35.3.11 B (page 6) and are certified for 90 days. Nothing in the rule authorizes practicum with participants who are not qualified patients: 7.35.3.14 (A) permits a practitioner to "sell or otherwise provide medical psilocybin products to qualified patients", and 7.35.3.19 speaks only of patients. So the practicum supply is bounded by the enrolled patient population, which in turn requires certifying clinicians, which requires the New Mexico module (see B2).

The only escape routes are two discretionary waiver provisions with no criteria, no procedure, and no time limit, at 7.35.3.10 D(2) (page 5) and 7.35.3.19 F (page 13), both reading in substance:

> "The department may otherwise waive, temporarily suspend, or reduce the practicum requirements for individuals applying for certification, in order to facilitate the certification of individuals trained by other government-approved programs, and to build the initial infrastructure of the program."

**Transcript note.** Both the launch bottleneck and possible responses were raised on July 17 and are not addressed in the published text. The afternoon committee transcript records: "The current indications really limit our patient pool and therefore placements and trainees getting through it"; "the current draft requires that all hours be held in an approved healing center, which I think can potentially be a bottleneck for rural trainees"; and a proposal that practicum "be operated on well patients, those without qualifying conditions." The morning board transcript records: "what's going to be presented in the training committee this afternoon is impossible to do adequate training without having the ability for people to use psilocybin in the training." 7.35.3.19 D is unchanged from the July 9 draft.

**Severity: BLOCKING.**

### B5. Healing center owners and employees are authorized only if "registered with the department", and no registration exists

**Section 7.35.3.14 (C) (page 9). NEW.**

> "(C) Healing centers: Owners and employees of healing centers who are registered with the department may purchase medical psilocybin products from permitted producers, may possess medical psilocybin products, and may sell or otherwise administer those products to qualified patients in administration sessions conducted at the healing center or other approved locations, provided that such individuals are also designated to engage in each activity by the healing center."

**The concern.** The rule contains no registration process for healing center owners or employees. 7.35.3.11 A(11) (page 5) requires an applicant healing center to submit "A list of all owners or members of the board of directors, including corresponding contact information", and A(2) requires names of "any affiliated practitioners or facilitators", but neither is described as registration and neither covers employees generally. As written, the condition precedent to lawful possession by healing center staff cannot be satisfied.

**Severity: BLOCKING.**

## Part 3. Training and education: MATERIAL findings

### M1. Didactic hours increased after the July 17 vote to defer them

**Section 7.35.3.18 C (page 11). NEW.**

Published text:

> "C. Requirements for initial practitioner and facilitator certification: All practitioners and facilitators shall complete a psilocybin therapy module consisting of a minimum of 30 didactic hours and 5 hours of simulated patient experience, prior to applying for certification"

July 9 draft text:

> "C. Requirements for initial practitioner and facilitator certification: All practitioners and facilitators shall complete a psilocybin therapy module consisting of a minimum of 25 didactic hours, prior to applying for certification"

**The concern.** The July 9 draft set 25 didactic hours plus a 5-hour simulated patient experience described at C(13) as "in addition to the 25 didactic hours". The published rule sets 30 didactic hours plus 5 hours of simulated patient experience. The core module minimum rose by five didactic hours. Three topics were also added to the required list: "(3) Trauma-Informed Care", "(11) Dosing", and "(13) Non-ordinary states of consciousness".

On July 17 the Advisory Board voted on the record to send these numbers back to the Training and Education Committee. The morning board transcript records the motion as: "we can move to a vote on to defer the training and education practicum and didactic hours to the training and education committee for them to solve to reassess and re-come together and have time to come up with those updated standards", followed by "I see seven hands." The published rule sets didactic hours at a number that is higher than the number in the draft that existed on the day of the vote.

This should be corrected in any statement that the didactic hours were left unchanged. They were not. This is the clearest additional instance of the pattern the board asked to be checked.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M2. A new practicum entry gate appeared that was never discussed at either July 17 meeting

**Section 7.35.3.19 A (page 12). NEW.**

> "An individual who seeks to become certified as a practitioner or facilitator shall participate in supervised practice training, otherwise referred to as a 'practicum', after completing at least half of the didactic requirements and all of the simulated patient requirements of the educational requirements."

**The concern.** The July 9 draft contained no prerequisite for entering practicum. This clause is new. It is ambiguous in operation: "half of the didactic requirements" could mean half of the 30-hour therapy module in 7.35.3.18 C, or half of the aggregate of 7.35.3.18 A, B or C, and D or E. Programs must sequence their curriculum against a number the rule does not define.

**Transcript note.** The question was asked directly at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting: "I was curious at what point is the student eligible to start practicum ... Did they have to have been through a certain amount of didactic first or is it didactic needs to be complete and then they start their practicum?" The answer recorded from the chair was "in full didactic is completed in full before practicum begins", and a subsequent commenter advocated interleaving didactic and practicum. Neither the "half" threshold nor the simulated-patient prerequisite appears anywhere in either transcript. This is text that was never discussed at either meeting.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M3. The two 40-hour practicum waivers set different group-session minimums

**Sections 7.35.3.10 D(1) (page 5) and 7.35.3.19 G(4) (page 13). NEW in both.**

Out-of-jurisdiction waiver, 7.35.3.10 D(1):

> "shall not be required to complete the full practicum hours required by New Mexico, but shall demonstrate completion of at least 40 hours of contact time with a minimum of two separate individual sessions and two separate group sessions for preparation, administration, and integrative therapy with psilocybin treatment."

General waiver, 7.35.3.19 G(4):

> "Demonstrates completion of at least 40 hours of contact time through logs or other records of the sessions, including: (a) A minimum of two separate individual sessions including the appointments for preparation, administration and integration; and (b) A minimum of one group session including the appointments for preparation, administration, and integration."

**The concern.** Same 40 hours, different session floors: two group sessions in one provision, one group session in the other. An applicant who has one group session qualifies under 7.35.3.19 G but not under 7.35.3.10 D, and the two provisions overlap for anyone who trained out of state and also graduated from a New Mexico-certified program.

**Diff note.** The July 9 draft used "or" in both places ("two separate individual sessions or two separate group sessions"). The published rule changed both to conjunctive requirements and, in doing so, made them inconsistent with each other.

**Transcript note.** The July 17 afternoon committee transcript records the department's description of the intent as matching: "this is to match the same thing that we're requiring even in-state up until December 31st of 27 is that 40 contact hours with patients." The published text does not match.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M4. The December 31, 2027 practicum waiver may cancel itself

**Sections 7.35.3.19 G(3) (page 13) and 7.35.3.17 B (page 11). NEW.**

7.35.3.19 G conditions the waiver on the applicant:

> "(3) Graduates from an educational program that the department certifies by December 31, 2027 or that the department has included on the department-approved list of educational programs by December 31, 2027; and"

7.35.3.17 B states:

> "Students who utilize this option shall complete all other requirements for the educational program, including the New Mexico Module, certifications, simulated patient, and practicum requirements to graduate from the educational program."

**The concern.** If completing the practicum requirements is a condition of graduation, then any applicant who satisfies G(3) has already completed the full practicum and has nothing left to waive. The waiver is only operative if a program may graduate a student before practicum is finished. The rule does not say which is correct. 7.35.3.17 A treats graduation and practicum completion as separate events ("after graduation and after practicum hours are completed"), which points the other way. Every applicant relying on the December 31, 2027 pathway, and every program advising them, needs this resolved before the deadline.

A second problem in the same subsection: G(3)'s second branch refers to "the department-approved list of educational programs", but the only list the rule creates is the list of programs from other jurisdictions in 7.35.3.10 A. The cross-reference points at a list that by definition does not contain New Mexico programs.

**Diff note.** The July 9 equivalent conditioned the waiver on completion of didactic requirements "through an educational program subsequently certified by the department by June 30, 2027" and required the 40 hours to be "conducted in any jurisdiction where psilocybin therapy is legal." The published text drops the jurisdiction clause. Combined with 7.35.3.19 D, which requires that "All practicum hours shall take place in an approved healing center or other approved location", it is now unclear whether the 40 waiver hours may be earned outside New Mexico at all.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M5. The third-party evaluation deferral expires on the same day the deferral becomes available

**Section 7.35.3.12 B(1) (page 8). NEW.**

> "(1) Applications for certification as an education program that are submitted on or before December 31, 2027 may be approved without an accompanying third-party evaluation report, provided that the applicant submits the third-party evaluation by December 31, 2027. If the third-party evaluation is not submitted, the certification will be rescinded."

**The concern.** The eligibility window and the compliance deadline are the same date. A program applying in November 2027 gets weeks; a program applying on December 31, 2027 gets zero days. The accommodation shrinks to nothing exactly as it is most needed. There is also an unresolved conflict with 7.35.3.12 A, which lists the report at A(20) among items a "complete application shall include, at minimum". And rescission under B(1) is not tied to the notice, hearing, or appeal machinery in 7.35.3.27.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M6. The out-of-jurisdiction program list is empty at launch and can only be populated retroactively

**Section 7.35.3.10 A (page 4). NEW.**

> "(2) Educational programs shall be added to the list when an application for certification as a certifying clinician, practitioner, or facilitator is submitted and approved based on the applicant's completion of the out-of-state educational program."

> "(4) Educational programs that are added to the department list of approved psilocybin educational programs from other jurisdictions shall remain on the list for two years"

> "(5) Consistent with this rule, educational programs that are included on the list of approved educational programs from other jurisdictions do not hold certification under this rule, and shall not be entitled to an administrative appeal in the event that the educational program is removed from the list."

**The concern.** A prospective student cannot check the list before enrolling, because the list is built only after some other individual has already applied and been approved on the basis of that program. Every early applicant must independently prove curriculum equivalency under B(2)(a)(i) or B(2)(b)(i). Listings then expire after two years regardless of the program's continuing status in its home jurisdiction, and a program removed from the list has no recourse. A(3)'s illustrative clause is also fixed to a future date: "including but not limited to psilocybin educational programs approved by the states of Oregon and Colorado as of December 31, 2027", which leaves programs approved by those states after that date outside the illustration.

**Transcript note.** The July 17 afternoon committee transcript records a training operator asking exactly this: "We have a number of students reaching out to us and asking if they can enroll into our Oregon or Colorado ... programs can transfer that training later on into New Mexico and as I mentioned, As of now, we don't have a clear answer." The published mechanism does not produce a clear advance answer.

**Diff note.** The July 9 reciprocity section allowed an applicant to rely on documented approval by another governmental agency plus a curriculum equivalency showing, and gave listed programs a renewal path through a department curriculum review. The retroactive-population mechanism, the two-year expiry, and the no-appeal clause are all new.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M7. After January 1, 2028 an individual applicant must produce a third-party evaluation that only a program can commission

**Section 7.35.3.10 B(2)(a)(ii) and B(2)(b)(ii) (page 4). NEW.**

> "(ii) For all applications received on or after January 1, 2028, documentation of a third-party evaluation of the curriculum demonstrating such equivalency"

**The concern.** This obligation falls on the individual applying for certification. 7.35.3.16 A places the duty to engage an evaluation team on "A psilocybin educational program", and 7.35.3.16 requires a conflict-free team of no fewer than three credentialed evaluators. An individual graduate of an Oregon or Colorado program has no standing to commission such a review of their alma mater's curriculum and no realistic means to pay for one. Unless a listing already exists under 7.35.3.10 A, the out-of-jurisdiction pathway effectively closes on January 1, 2028.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M8. Programs cannot replace an instructor until the department approves, and no deadline binds the department

**Section 7.35.3.15 A (page 9). CARRIED OVER, with a partial NEW fix elsewhere.**

> "A department-certified psilocybin educational program shall notify the department, through the department-approved electronic system designated by the department, of any of the following changes, and shall not implement the change until written approval is issued by the department:"

> "(5) Any change in instructional staff, including submission of curricula vitae or résumés for all newly added instructors."

**The concern.** The list also covers ownership changes, physical locations, and "(4) Any modification, termination, or addition of practicum site agreements". No provision sets any time limit for the department to act on any of them. If an instructor resigns mid-cohort, the program may not substitute a replacement until written approval issues, with no outside date. The published rule added a 60-day decision requirement to 7.35.3.15 B for curriculum approval applications ("The department shall notify the applicant within 60 calendar days"), which was not in the July 9 draft, and did not add one to subsection A.

There is also duplication: A(1) requires prior approval of curriculum revisions, and B establishes a separate curriculum approval application process. The rule does not say which governs a revision.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M9. The grounds for denying an educational program application were removed

**Section 7.35.3.12 D (page 8) and 7.35.3.15 (pages 9 to 10). CHANGED, by deletion.**

The July 9 draft contained an explicit subsection C, "Application denial: The Department may deny an application if: (1) The submitted curriculum does not comply with the Department's required curriculum standards; (2) The applicant fails to submit all required documentation or submits incomplete materials; or (3) Program instructors, staff, or representatives have made false or misleading statements to the Department, to students, or to the public."

No equivalent appears in the published rule. 7.35.3.12 D states only that "If the department denies an application, the department shall provide notice of the denial in accordance with this rule."

**The concern.** Applicants for educational program certification now have no stated criteria against which to prepare, and appellants under 7.35.3.27 have no stated standard against which to argue. The corresponding grounds for disciplinary action do survive at 7.35.3.27 B, but those govern action against an existing certificate, not denial of an application.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M10. The third-party evaluator qualification standard may be unmeetable, and its frequency is undefined

**Section 7.35.3.16 A and B (page 10). CARRIED OVER.**

> "The evaluation team shall consist of no fewer than three individuals who collectively meet all competency requirements set forth in this section."

> "Each evaluator shall possess at least three years of professional experience in one or more of the following fields, and the evaluation team as a whole shall demonstrate expertise covering all listed domains: (1) Psilocybin therapy practice, supported by a master's or doctoral degree in counseling, therapy, social work, public health, behavioral health, or a closely related field"

**The concern.** Every New Mexico educational program must find at least three qualified people, covering all three domains, with no financial or governance relationship to the program, one of whom has three years of professional psilocybin therapy practice plus a graduate degree. The pool of people with three years of regulated psilocybin therapy practice is small and concentrated in the same operators likely to be advising or teaching in New Mexico programs, which is precisely what 7.35.3.16 C(2)(a) disqualifies ("Participation in the design, development, or teaching of the curriculum being evaluated").

Frequency is also undefined. 7.35.3.16 A refers to "the evaluations required under this rule" in the plural. 7.35.3.12 A(20) requires one at initial application. 7.35.3.12 C requires "a complete renewal application packet" every two years without saying whether a fresh evaluation is part of it. Programs cannot budget for this.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M11. The 10-hour mentoring obligation is unfunded, survives the program, and is not tied to certification

**Section 7.35.3.17 A (pages 10 to 11). CARRIED OVER.**

> "All educational programs shall provide each practitioner and facilitator student with a minimum of 10 hours of mentoring sessions after graduation and after practicum hours are completed. ... These mentoring sessions may not have additional associated charges beyond the cost of the educational program itself. The 10 hours are valid for up to 12 months after practicum hours are completed."

**The concern.** Three problems. First, the obligation extends up to 12 months past graduation but 7.35.3.26 (page 16) allows a program to "voluntarily relinquish its certification, at any time", and 7.35.3.12 C allows a certification to lapse at renewal; the rule provides no substitute mentor and no notice to affected graduates. Second, mentoring is not listed among the certification application requirements at 7.35.3.9 C, and practicum must be "completed prior to applying for certification" under 7.35.3.19 A, so a graduate may be certified and practicing before, or entirely without, the mentoring. Third, the cost is absorbed into tuition with no mechanism to recognize it.

**Transcript note.** This was criticized on the record at the July 17 afternoon committee meeting: "the current drafts, 10 hours of mentoring never require that the permittee has seen a client. So consultation groups in Colorado have allowed completion of these hours by attendance alone." The recommendation offered was "20 to 30 hours of supervision or consultation during the permit period, in addition to the practicum", with sign-off conditioned on presenting two cases. The published text is unchanged from July 9 on this point.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M12. The test-out price cap assumes per-module pricing that may not exist

**Section 7.35.3.17 B (page 11). Substance CARRIED OVER from a July 9 bullet list; converted to rule text.**

> "Educational programs shall offer an option for a student to complete the evaluation for each educational module (not including the New Mexico module) without attending the lessons or classes for the module. The cost to complete the evaluation for each module shall not be greater than one-fourth of the normal price of each of the educational modules."

**The concern.** A program that charges one tuition for a single integrated course has no "normal price of each of the educational modules", so the cap has no referent and the program cannot demonstrate compliance. The provision effectively compels per-module pricing without saying so. Separately, this subsection sits in tension with 7.35.3.16 E(3), which requires the third-party team to assess "the reliability, validity, and fidelity of implementation of the curriculum", while the rule requires programs to allow students to skip instruction in every module except the New Mexico module.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M13. Required curriculum content omits the indications the board's own committees flagged

**Section 7.35.3.18 C (pages 11 to 12). Topic list partially NEW.**

The published list of required topics for the practitioner and facilitator module is: overview of responsibilities, 42 CFR part 2, Trauma-Informed Care, psychedelic emergencies and medical monitoring, pharmacology, set and setting, ethics, patient-centered care, preparation and integration sessions, administration session, dosing, de-escalation, non-ordinary states of consciousness, self-care, research and data collection, the 5-hour simulated patient experience, and an evaluation.

**The concern.** There is no required content on end-of-life care, on suicidality or crisis screening, or on substance use disorder, although all three are within the program's qualifying conditions. This affects every practitioner and facilitator who will treat those populations.

**Transcript note.** All three were raised on July 17 at the afternoon committee meeting: "if we cannot create a specific version, certification requirement for people to provide care to vulnerable, specifically uniquely vulnerable populations ... then I think it's that much more important that we have specific didactic requirements for those indications ... that would be substance use disorder, end of life, suicidality, PTSD, that that be incorporated into curriculum requirements." Also recorded: "there was a pretty strong feeling that end of life is a distinct enough set that really does need specific training", with a suggestion of 10 to 15 hours at core, and a training operator's account of "nine competencies and effectively 19 hours of live instruction" for end-of-life. Three topics were added to the module between July 9 and July 23 (Trauma-Informed Care, Dosing, Non-ordinary states of consciousness). None of the three indication-specific topics was.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M14. Practicum patient counts may be cumulative or overlapping, and the rule does not say which

**Section 7.35.3.19 A and B (pages 12 to 13). CARRIED OVER.**

A requires "a minimum of 14 different patients over a minimum of eight different administration and same-day sessions", broken into "(1) A minimum of six different patients during a minimum of six individual administration day sessions; and (2) A minimum of two different group administration day sessions with four or more patients in each group administration day session."

B separately requires "a minimum of six different patients during individual sessions" plus "at least one group preparatory and one group integration session with a minimum of four or more patients."

**The concern.** A's internal arithmetic is consistent: 6 individual patients plus 8 group patients equals 14, across 6 plus 2 equals 8 sessions. What is not stated is whether B's six individual patients and two group sessions may be the same patients and the same cohorts as A's. Depending on the reading, a student must reach either 14 patients or as many as 28. Programs cannot design placements, and the department cannot audit records under 7.35.3.17 C(2)(d), without knowing which.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M15. Facilitator scope of work conflicts with the practicum, the staffing ratios, and the possession authority

**Sections 7.35.3.13 B (page 8), 7.35.3.14 (B) (page 9), 7.35.3.19 (pages 12 to 13), 7.35.3.20 H(5) (page 14). NEW.**

> "B. Facilitators; scope of work: A facilitator is authorized to work alongside a practitioner during medical psilocybin services. A facilitator works under the direct supervision of a practitioner, and provides peer support to qualified patients, as well as logistical and administrative support to the practitioner and to the healing center. A facilitator shall not perform any patient care outside this scope, unless another license held by the facilitator permits it."

**The concern.** Three conflicts. First, 7.35.3.19 requires facilitator students to complete 100 practicum hours including "supervised facilitation of administration day sessions" and, at 7.35.3.19 B, to "conduct (when licensure allows) preparation or integration sessions"; a facilitator holds no licensure under 7.35.3.9 F, so the practicum trains facilitators for activity that 7.35.3.13 B may forbid. Second, 7.35.3.20 H(5) permits one practitioner for every eight patients in group sessions while requiring one facilitator per two patients, so up to four facilitators would be under the "direct supervision" of one practitioner who is simultaneously responsible for eight patients. Third, 7.35.3.11 B allows "A practitioner or facilitator" to apply for certification of an other approved location independently, and 7.35.3.14 (B) allows facilitators to possess and provide products, neither of which fits within "peer support ... as well as logistical and administrative support."

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

## Part 4. Everything else: MATERIAL findings

### M16. Outdoor "other approved locations" do not require a first aid kit or an AED, while healing centers do

**Section 7.35.3.11 A(22)(e) and B(10) (page 6). NEW.**

For healing centers conducting outdoor sessions, A(22) includes:

> "(e) If the natural environment setting is 15 minutes or more away from emergency medical service response or a hospital, urgent care, or other emergency medical services, then there must be a basic first aid kit and an AED on site;"

The parallel provision for other approved locations, B(10), contains subparagraphs (a) through (d), including the two-trained-persons requirement, but stops there. It has no equipment clause.

**The concern.** Other approved locations are, by 7.35.3.11 B, the ones used when "a qualified patient is unable to be physically transported to a healing center", "for purposes of improving patient access in rural and frontier counties, or to enable treatment to occur in a natural environment setting." The setting with the least infrastructure and the greatest distance from emergency services is the one exempted from carrying an AED and a first aid kit. Nothing in either transcript discusses this distinction.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M17. Complainants lose the confidentiality assurance and must use certified mail

**Section 7.35.3.24 (page 15). CHANGED.**

> "The complaint should be in writing, addressed to the medical psilocybin program, and submitted by U.S. certified mail or through the electronic system designated by the department electronic system. ... A complaint shall include the name and contact information for the complainant; and a statement of the nature of the complaint and any individuals who may be involved."

The July 9 draft provided for "Name and contact information for the complainant which will remain confidential."

**The concern.** The confidentiality clause is gone. Anonymous complaints are not permitted, since the name and contact information are mandatory. The affected complainants include qualified patients complaining about a practitioner and students complaining about a program, both of whom are in a dependent relationship with the subject of the complaint. The sentence also contains a duplication: "through the electronic system designated by the department electronic system."

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M18. Healing centers must maintain a patient list, and the department may interview patients

**Sections 7.35.3.20 A (page 13) and 7.35.3.21 A (page 15). NEW.**

> "A. A healing center and a registrant of another approved location shall maintain a list of all qualified patients who are intended to utilize, and who have utilized, the location for administration sessions."

> "In conducting assessments, the department may review documentation, inspect premises and equipment, and interview staff and patients."

**The concern.** The July 9 draft required an approved location to maintain "a list of all certified individuals who will be utilizing or who have utilized the approved location". The published rule replaces the list of providers with a list of patients, including prospective patients. The July 9 draft's assessment provision contained no authority to interview patients; the published rule adds it, alongside "with or without prior notice". Neither change was discussed at either July 17 meeting. The existing protection at 7.35.3.21 A is limited: "except in emergency circumstances, the department or its designee shall not conduct an on-site assessment during a patient session or at a patient's home without prior written consent from the patient(s)", which addresses timing and place but not the interview authority itself.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M19. The certifying clinician attestation retains the benefit-risk language that drew a July 17 objection

**Section 7.35.3.8 B(8)(c) (page 2). CARRIED OVER in substance.**

> "(c) The certifying clinician has evaluated the medical appropriateness of the patient's proposed enrollment in the New Mexico psilocybin program, and that, in the clinician's professional opinion, the potential health benefits of the medical use of psilocybin would likely outweigh health risks for the patient"

**The concern.** This is an attestation about therapeutic benefit, not solely about medical clearance. The July 17 morning board transcript records a public comment raising two consequences: reimbursement and liability. On reimbursement: "if I were the federal government overseeing our Medicaid system, or if I were payer, I would look at the regulation and say, actually, this encounter only happened for the prescription of a Schedule I substance and deny the claim or claw it back if it had already been made and came up in an audit." On liability: "the requirement to provide, you know, a risk and benefit analysis does increase the liability from the provider ... I think that if it were revised to just require an assessment of medical risk, one, that's going to create less risk of clawbacks and two, you know, less other professional liability on the certifying clinician." The published text was not narrowed. This is a drafting question about the scope of the attestation and is independent of whether a controlled substance number is required.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M20. The renewal window can close before the department decides, with no continued-validity provision

**Sections 7.35.3.9 A(1) and B (pages 2 to 3), 7.35.3.11 C (page 7), 7.35.3.12 C (page 8). CARRIED OVER.**

> "B. Renewal of certification; submittal deadline: Applicants for renewal of certification shall submit a completed renewal application packet through the electronic system designated by the department no more than 60 days and no less than 30 days prior to the expiration of their current certification."

> "(1) Once the department has received a completed application, it will review the application and render a decision within 30 calendar days."

**The concern.** A certificant who files on the last permitted day, 30 days before expiration, may receive a decision on the day of expiration. If the department takes the full 30 days, or if it requests more information, the certification lapses. Nothing provides that a timely-filed renewal keeps the certification in effect pending decision. This affects certifying clinicians, practitioners, facilitators, healing centers, and educational programs alike, and for an educational program a lapse also strands enrolled students and the mentoring obligation in M11.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

---

### M21. The re-application clause reads as a deadline rather than a waiting period

**Sections 7.35.3.9 A(5) and A(6) (page 2), repeated at 7.35.3.10 C (page 4), 7.35.3.11 D (page 7), and 7.35.3.12 D (page 8). CARRIED OVER.**

> "(5) If the application is denied, the applicant may re-apply within six months."
> "(6) An applicant who is denied a second time may not re-apply for six months from the denial."

**The concern.** Read literally, (5) creates a six-month window after which a once-denied applicant may never re-apply, and (6) creates a six-month waiting period after a second denial. The two clauses use "within six months" and "for six months from the denial" to mean opposite things in adjacent sentences. Whichever reading the department intends, applicants will read them differently, and the language is repeated verbatim in four separate sections. A related but differently worded rule appears at 7.35.3.15 B: "An applicant whose application has been denied may resubmit a corrected application within six months. No applicant may resubmit an application more than two times following an initial denial."

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

---

### M22. Other approved locations have a 90-day term, no renewal path, and no decision deadline

**Section 7.35.3.11 B and D (pages 6 to 7). NEW as rule text.**

> "Certification of other approved locations shall be effective on the date of department issuance and shall be valid for 90 days."

**The concern.** 7.35.3.11 C provides a renewal process for healing centers only. There is no renewal mechanism for other approved locations, so each 90-day period requires a fresh application with the full 13-item packet in B. No provision states how quickly the department must decide such an application; the only timeline in D is for notice of denial, "within 30 calendar days". These locations exist for patients who are "unable to be physically transported to a healing center" and for "other reasons of medical necessity", which includes end-of-life patients. A process with no decision deadline is a direct access problem for that population.

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### M23. Two review tracks for denied patient applicants have inconsistent scope

**Sections 7.35.3.8 D (page 2), 7.35.3.25 A and B (page 16), 7.35.3.27 C(7) (page 17). CARRIED OVER, with NEW additions.**

7.35.3.8 D limits the informal track: "A person whose application for patient enrollment is denied for failure to complete an application or failure to meet a submittal requirement of this rule may request a record review".

7.35.3.25 A is unlimited: "An applicant for patient enrollment whose application has been denied may request an informal administrative review from the department."

7.35.3.27 C(7) allows a hearing for an applicant "whose application is denied for any reason other than failure to submit a completed application or failure to meet a submittal requirement of this rule."

**The concern.** 7.35.3.8 D and 7.35.3.27 C(7) together create a clean split. 7.35.3.25 A does not respect that split and offers the informal track to all denied applicants. 7.35.3.25 B(1) also newly extends the informal track to "a patient whose enrollment has been immediately suspended", a category created by 7.35.3.27 A, which is separately entitled to a hearing under 7.35.3.27 C(1). The rule does not say which track controls or whether they run in parallel, and 7.35.3.25 E provides that "there shall be no right to judicial review of a decision by the administrative review committee." Two different terms are also used for the same proceeding: 7.35.3.8 D and 7.35.3.25 D(1) say "record review", while 7.35.3.25's title and body say "informal administrative review".

**Severity: MATERIAL.**

### N5. Disciplinary timeline for an immediately suspended certificant

**Section 7.35.3.27 (pages 16 to 19). Immediate suspension authority is NEW.**

7.35.3.27 A newly provides that "The department may also immediately suspend enrollment or certification if the department determines that such action is necessary to protect the health or safety of a patient or the general public." Following suspension, F allows the hearing to be held up to 60 calendar days after the request, O(2) allows the hearing officer 30 calendar days after the last submission to recommend, and O(3) allows the secretary 45 calendar days after receipt to decide. A suspended certificant may therefore be out of practice for more than 135 days before a final decision, with no expedited track. NOTE.
