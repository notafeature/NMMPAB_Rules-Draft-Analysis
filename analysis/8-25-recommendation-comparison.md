# The August 25 revised proposed rule against the recommendation's August 21 position

The committee's recommendation has stood alone since the July 23 publication was set aside, compared against nothing. The August 25 publication ends that posture: this file runs the recommendation, at the position recorded in `analysis/8-21-committee-extraction.md`, against the operative published text, and states what the department took, what it took in altered form, and what it declined. The summary of the recommended rules itself is not held in this repository; the recommendation's figures are cited to the August 21 record of the document presented at that meeting.

## Sources

| Position | Source |
|---|---|
| The recommendation, August 21 position | `analysis/8-21-committee-extraction.md`: 80 didactic hours with minimums in nine content areas; a staged practicum of 114 hours for licensed providers or 102 for facilitators closing in case presentation and consultation |
| The published text | `docs/documents/rules-draft-2026-08-25-published.pdf`, cited by section and page; definitions in `docs/documents/rules-7.35.2-amendments-2026-08-25-published.pdf` |

The department's own August 21 side-by-side put a "DOH Proposed Rule" column beside the subcommittee's figures. The published text substantially realizes that column: 65 general didactic hours plus five role-specific plus 10 simulated patient, totaling 80, and a practicum of 100 or 120 hours with case presentation inside the hours. That sheet remains meeting record, not a standing position; it is noted here because the published text now makes it the department's enacted drafting choice.

## Declined

- **The role name licensed provider.** The published text uses practitioner throughout, and the amended definition rewrites the term while keeping the name: "a clinician who is certified by the department to provide medical psilocybin integrative therapy, supervise facilitators" (7.35.2.7 as amended, AMEND p. 3). "Licensed provider" appears nowhere in the three August 25 documents.
- **The role name medical screener.** The published text keeps certifying clinician, 54 occurrences, and anchors the name in a new definition carrying the controlled substances number requirement (7.35.2.7 as amended, AMEND p. 1). "Medical screener" appears nowhere in the three documents.
- **Per-area hour minimums in the didactic module.** The recommendation set minimums in nine content areas summing to 63 of its 80 hours. The rule lists 26 topics "at a minimum" with no hours attached to any topic (7.35.3.18 (C)(1), p. 12). This is the position the department stated on August 21: content areas in the rule, hour scrutiny at program application.
- **The staged practicum and its totals.** The recommendation built 114 hours for licensed providers and 102 for facilitators from named stages: well participants 24, co-facilitation 24, group work 12, provider supervisory 12, and a 42-hour supervised practice and consultation stage. The rule keeps the July 23 architecture and totals: 120 for practitioners and 100 for facilitators, from 80 administration-day hours, 20 preparation-and-integration hours, and 20 practitioner supervision hours (7.35.3.19 (A), (B), (D), pp. 13 to 14). No stage is named for well participants; on August 21 the department stated the statute does not allow practicum work with individuals who do not have a qualifying condition.
- **The 18-hour consultation group.** The recommendation required at least 18 hours of case presentation and consultation with program faculty, in a group, with two cases presented, as a stage of the practicum. The rule requires no consultation group and attaches no hours to consultation; see the altered case-presentation requirement below.

## Taken in altered form

- **The 80-hour didactic total.** The recommendation's 80 hours and the rule's 80 hours match in total and differ in composition. The recommendation: 80 module hours with a two-hour minimum for role playing and/or simulated patient experience among its nine content areas. The rule: a 65-hour therapy module with at least one third in person, a five-hour role-specific module, and 10 hours of simulated patient experience (7.35.3.18 (C), (D), (E), pp. 12 to 13). Against the July 23 text, this is the recommendation's total winning at double the published didactic floor; against the recommendation, the simulated-patient share is five times larger.
- **Two case presentations before certification.** The recommendation's competency checkpoint: two cases the student personally served, presented within an 18-hour consultation group with faculty, completed before full licensure. The rule: "the student shall present a minimum of two of the last four patients to the practicum supervisor as case presentations for evaluation, and must receive a passing grade on the case presentations to complete their practicum" (7.35.3.19 (C), p. 14). The two-case gate and the pass requirement survive; the audience shrinks from a consultation group to the practicum supervisor, the dedicated 18 hours disappear, and the hours sit inside the existing practicum totals.
- **Low-acuity work before high-acuity work.** The recommendation sequenced the practicum from lower-risk to higher-risk, with low-acuity patients in the co-facilitation stage and high-acuity care allowed after 48 hours. The rule orders the same direction with different machinery: "The initial 20 hours of administration day sessions shall be with patients who are determined by the supervising practitioner to be low-risk for adverse events or other complications" (7.35.3.19 (A)(3), p. 14).
- **Compensation partway through training.** The recommendation's table marked stages one and two uncompensated and later stages compensated, the boundary at 48 hours. The rule reaches a similar point through the healing center provisions: a student registered with a certified program who has completed at least 50 practicum hours is "deemed qualified to substitute as a facilitator, including for the purpose of billing" (7.35.3.20, p. 15).
- **Breadth of the didactic topics.** Much of the recommendation's content-area substance enters the rule as named topics without hours: trauma-informed care, cultural competencies, informed consent, touch and somatic awareness, equity and access, traditional and ceremonial practices, preparation and integration, dosing, de-escalation (7.35.3.18 (C)(1), p. 12). Two named concerns of the recommendation do not surface as topics: end-of-life and palliative care, whose nearest counterpart is "Education on the qualifying conditions and appropriate treatment practices," end-of-life care being a qualifying condition; and suicidality, whose nearest counterparts are the screening, safety, and crisis-adjacent topics. Neither phrase appears in the rule's topic list.

## Where the positions already agreed

- **Continuing education.** Eight hours every two years for certifying clinicians, 20 for practitioners and facilitators (7.35.3.18 (G), p. 13). Identical in both proposals on August 21 and unchanged.
- **The emergency response credential.** BLS, or CPR and AED, or New Mexico EMT licensure (7.35.3.18 (F), p. 13).
- **The New Mexico module**, required of every role with no exemption by reciprocity (7.35.3.18 (A), pp. 11 to 12).
- **The transition window.** Application and didactic completion by December 31, 2027 with a reduced practicum of 40 documented contact hours (7.35.3.19 (H), p. 14; 7.35.3.10, p. 5), matching the transition terms stated on August 21.

## Kept against the recommendation's absence

- **Mentoring.** The rule keeps the 10 post-graduation mentoring hours as a program obligation (7.35.3.17 (A), p. 11). The recommendation carried no mentoring line, placing its consultation inside the practicum instead. The rule now carries both a case-presentation gate inside the practicum and the mentoring hours after it.

## Standing consequences for the site

- Comparisons on the content pages now run recommendation against the August 25 text; the July 23 text is history.
- The certifying clinician / medical screener pair reverts to certifying clinician: the department kept the published term. The recommendation's proposed rename remains stated where the recommendation is described.
- The site's voice for the middle role follows the published text, practitioner, with licensed provider stated as the recommendation's proposed name. The August 21 record already established that the department's own materials kept practitioner; the published text confirms it.
