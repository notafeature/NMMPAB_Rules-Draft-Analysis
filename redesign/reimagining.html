Practicum requirements

The supervised practice a person must complete before they can be certified to work with patients.

An individual who seeks to become certified as a practitioner or facilitator shall participate in supervised practice training, otherwise referred to as a “practicum”, after completing at least half of the didactic requirements and all of the simulated patient requirements°New in this draft. 7.35.3.19 (A), published July 23, 2026, p. 12. Subsections B through G follow in the full text.

As published

A minimum of 100 hours for facilitators and 120 hours for practitioners, all of it inside an approved healing center or other approved location.

The text does not say how a student may lawfully sit with a patient during these hours.

As recommended to the committee

A staged practicum of 62 to 72 hours: practice with well participants first, then low-acuity cases beside an experienced facilitator, then group work, then lead-chair hours.

Supervision rises from 10 hours to 20, tied to two presented cases.

What the difference means for a person Under the published text, a working clinician gives up roughly $18,000 to $24,000 in earnings to sit the 120 hours, before tuition and travel, and a rural candidate must reach an approved center for every hour. Under the recommendation, the hours nearly halve and the early stages can happen where the candidate lives. Dr. Anne Metz gave the cost estimate to the committee on July 17; the staged model is hers.
Open · in committee
The board sent these hours back on July 17. The committee's recommendation is being written and reviewed now; it meets August 21, seven days before the hearing.
Who this reaches
Every person who wants to become a facilitator or practitioner, and every clinic deciding whether training is affordable enough to offer.
Defect as written
The practicum requires students to work with patients, and no provision authorizes a student to possess or administer psilocybin. The three defects, stated in full.
