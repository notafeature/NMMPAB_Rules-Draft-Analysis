New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Program

Routes to a permit

Updated August 24, 2026.

Pick where you start below and follow every step to each of the three permits, certifying clinician / medical screener, Licensed Provider, facilitator, as the last published text of July 23, since set aside, sets them out. Where the committee recommendation would change a step, the change is shown directly beneath it.

● Settled In the last published text, not changed on the record.
◆ Open Still moving: the practicum and didactic hours, deferred to the Training and Education Committee by a 7-0 board vote on July 17. The text published July 23, since set aside, carried the practicum hours forward unchanged and raised the shared didactic module from 25 hours to 30. The recommendation stands at the committee's August 21 position, in the department's hands for the revised proposed rules; the department decides what enters the hearing text.
▼ Contested Kept in the rule over a named, on-record objection: Dr. Anne Metz, June 25 committee meeting.

Pathway to the Facilitator permit

No professional license required. An end-of-life doula enters here, on the same terms as any other applicant. The rule gives a doula no permit or scope of their own; it allows any other individual to be present at an administration session on each patient's prior written consent, at 7.35.3.20 (D).

  1. 1

    Apply for certification. No professional license required.

    published rule 7.35.3.9 (F), p.3

  2. 2

    Certifications: HIPAA, plus BLS, or (CPR and AED), or New Mexico EMT licensure. Attestation you are not a registered sex offender.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.12 · 7.35.3.9, p.3

  3. 3

    Approved Facilitator training. Begins with the New Mexico Module, the one module you cannot test out of.

    If the committee recommendation is adopted80 hours in place of these 35, with minimum hours in nine content areas. The recommendation states its 80 across nine content areas including 2 hours of simulated patient work, so it is a module total and compares directly against the published 40, which is these 35 didactic hours plus the 5-hour simulated patient experience. The New Mexico module stays required of every role, with no exemption by reciprocity.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.11 · 7.35.3.17 (B), p.11

  4. 4

    Practicum at a healing center or other approved location. Published rule: 100 hours.

    ◆ Open

    Deferred to the Training and Education Committee by a 7-0 board vote July 17; the recommendation stands at the committee's August 21 position.

    If the committee recommendation is adopted102 hours, staged: 24 with well participants, 24 co-facilitating with a department-permitted licensed provider, 12 of group work, and 42 of supervised practice on two cases, with an 18-hour case presentation and consultation group inside the total, and every stage spanning preparation, administration, and integration.

    The working model of these hours

    published rule 7.35.3.19 (A), pp.12-13

  5. 5

    Mentoring: 10 hours after graduation and after the practicum.

    If the committee recommendation is adoptedRemoved as a separate step: the recommendation closes the practicum with an 18-hour case presentation and consultation group inside the practicum total, with sign-off requiring two presented cases the permittee personally provided.

    published rule 7.35.3.17, p.10

  6. 6

    Apply to NMDOH. Certification is valid 2 years from approval.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.2

  7. 7

    Continuing education: 20 hours every 2 years. Keep BLS or CPR/AED current.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.12

Pathway to the Certifying Clinician / Medical Screener permit

Only with a NM Controlled Substance number. That requirement was contested and kept by the department on July 17; the gate is the number, not your license scope.

  1. 1

    Hold a current NM professional license (for example MD, NP).

    The published rule names license examples; it does not describe a diagnosing scope.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.3

  2. 2

    Approved certifying clinician / medical screener training: an 8-hour module covering pharmacology, 42 CFR Part 2, diagnosis of qualifying conditions, medical clearance, monitoring, and data-collection requirements.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.11

  3. 3

    Hold a New Mexico Controlled Substance number (state number, not the federal DEA number).

    Contested through June and July, and kept by the department on July 17, which also revised the certifying-clinician evaluation language. It stands in the last published text and is not changed by the August 21 recommendation.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.3 · July 17 update

  4. 4

    No practicum.

    The practicum section applies to Licensed Providers and Facilitators only; the certifying clinician / medical screener's packet lists no practicum item.

    published rule 7.35.3.19, p.12 · 7.35.3.9, p.3

  5. 5

    Apply to NMDOH. Certification is valid 2 years from approval.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.2

  6. 6

    Continuing education: 8 CME hours every 2 years.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.12

These verdicts summarize the rows for this group in the eligibility tables.

Pathway by reciprocity: Licensed Provider or Facilitator

Enter by reciprocity, as Licensed Provider or Facilitator.

  1. 1

    Trained and licensed outside New Mexico. Your program must be on NMDOH's approved reciprocity list, which names Oregon and Colorado programs, or you must show your program has other governmental approval and a curriculum substantially equivalent to New Mexico's.

    published rule 7.35.3.10, p.4 · 7.35.3.19, p.12

  2. 2

    Complete the New Mexico Module.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.11 · 7.35.3.10, p.4

  3. 3

    Apply by the waiver deadline for a reduced practicum: at least 40 hours of contact time instead of the full practicum.

    The published rule sets this deadline at December 31, 2027. The July 9 draft still read December 31, 2026; the board moved it without objection that day and the published text now carries it. Board chair Ian Dunn noted that date is a legislative backstop, not the target date.

    published rule 7.35.3.10 (D), p.5 · July 9 meeting transcript

  4. 4

    NMDOH may further reduce the practicum requirement at its discretion, to build the program's initial infrastructure.

    The published rule sets no end date for this discretion, at 7.35.3.19 (F). The related waiver deadline is December 31, 2027.

    published rule 7.35.3.10 (D), p.5 · July 9 meeting transcript

  5. 5

    Apply as Licensed Provider or Facilitator. The reciprocity application packet lists items for those two permits only.

    ◆ Unresolved

    The published rule is inconsistent here. Reciprocity now sits inside 7.35.3.10, which names certifying clinicians as eligible to apply on the basis of a program from another jurisdiction, while the application items listed there are for practitioners and facilitators.

    published rule 7.35.3.10, p.4 · 7.35.3.19, p.12

Pathway to the Certifying Clinician / Medical Screener permit

The published rule names certifying clinicians as eligible under 7.35.3.10, but the application items listed there are for practitioners and facilitators. Unresolved in the draft.

  1. 1

    Hold a current NM professional license (for example MD, NP).

    The published rule names license examples; it does not describe a diagnosing scope.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.3

  2. 2

    Approved certifying clinician / medical screener training: an 8-hour module covering pharmacology, 42 CFR Part 2, diagnosis of qualifying conditions, medical clearance, monitoring, and data-collection requirements.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.11

  3. 3

    Hold a New Mexico Controlled Substance number (state number, not the federal DEA number).

    Contested through June and July, and kept by the department on July 17, which also revised the certifying-clinician evaluation language. It stands in the last published text and is not changed by the August 21 recommendation.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.3 · July 17 update

  4. 4

    No practicum.

    The practicum section applies to Licensed Providers and Facilitators only; the certifying clinician / medical screener's packet lists no practicum item.

    published rule 7.35.3.19, p.12 · 7.35.3.9, p.3

  5. 5

    Apply to NMDOH. Certification is valid 2 years from approval.

    published rule 7.35.3.9, p.2

  6. 6

    Continuing education: 8 CME hours every 2 years.

    published rule 7.35.3.18, p.12
