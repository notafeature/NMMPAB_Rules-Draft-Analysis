Record

The dated chain of the Training and Education rulemaking: every meeting, vote, and document version, with its source. This page remains when the rulemaking ends, as the account of how the rule was made.

Updated July 27, 2026.

Scheduled

AUG 14Advisory Board meets.
AUG 21Training and Education Committee meets, 9 to 11 AM, seven days before the hearing and after the text for it was published.
AUG 28Rule hearing on 7.35.3 NMAC. Public comment is taken and recorded there.

The chain

JUL 27The committee recommendation was submitted to the department as a plain-language summary of the recommended rules, resolving the practicum and didactic hours the board sent to committee on July 17. It recommends 80 didactic hours with minimums in nine content areas, a staged practicum of 60 hours for facilitators and 70 for licensed providers beginning with well participants, 20 hours of case presentation and consultation on two personally provided cases, the renaming of "practitioner" to "Licensed Provider," and a transition pathway through December 31, 2027. The document is not published on this site. Its figures beside the published text
JUL 23The department published the proposed rule, 19 pages, and the hearing was set for August 28. This is the first version with final section numbering, 7.35.3.1 through .28. The practicum hours were published unchanged at 100 and 120, six days after the board voted to send them to committee; the shared didactic module rose from 25 hours to 30, adding trauma-informed care, dosing, and non-ordinary states of consciousness. The published text also carries both waiver deadlines as December 31, 2027, the three certifying-clinician exam pathways read into the record on July 17, a new rule that the practicum may not begin until half the didactic and all simulated-patient requirements are complete, and a staffing ratio counting a qualified student at 50 practicum hours. The published text · typeset on this site · what changed, provision by provision
JUL 17Afternoon: the committee took up the deferred hours. Dr. Anne Metz presented her recommendations: retitle "practitioner," a single 84-hour didactic standard, a four-step scaffolded practicum of roughly 62 to 72 hours, and consultation sign-off tied to two presented cases. On the record: Denali Wilson asked that substance use disorder, end-of-life, suicidality, and PTSD be added as didactic requirements, and raised whether students could count toward the staffing ratio; Chris Caldwell argued for 19 or more live end-of-life instruction hours, and Larry Leeman supported roughly 10 to 15 in the core curriculum; several participants asked for a legacy practitioner pathway. Dominic Zurlo told the committee the department "really need[s] them sooner rather than later," with no deadline given. Committee transcript, unofficial, no speaker labels · the written recommendations
JUL 17Morning: the Advisory Board voted 7-0 to send the practicum and didactic hours, with the definitions, to the Training and Education Committee. Dominic Zurlo said that after taking the earlier concerns to the Secretary of Health and the department's attorneys, the certifying-clinician controlled-substance number stays in the rule the department puts forward; the board did not vote to remove it, and Chris Peskuski restated his objection that it is an access bottleneck placing liability on a clinician who is not in the room. The department read in revised exam language with three ways to meet the in-person requirement. Larry Leeman noted federal rescheduling to Schedule III, which he considers likely by mid-2027, could reopen the requirement, and the board asked the department to find how many New Mexico providers hold prescriptive authority without the state number. Board transcript, unofficial, no speaker labels · the verbatim exam language
JUL 16The End-of-Life committee raised specialization as an endorsement layered on a core permit, with end-of-life care as the worked example. Nothing in the published rule creates one.
JUL 9The Advisory Board resolved reciprocity without objection, extending both waiver deadlines to December 31, 2027, and voted to table the practicum and didactic hours for the committee. The controlled-substance number was sent back to the department to weigh alternatives. Also on the record: "diagnostic clinician" was renamed "certifying clinician" throughout the draft, AED certification was added alongside BLS and CPR in individual training with EMT certification as an outdoor-location staffing alternative, and Keenan Ryan appeared as the new Medicaid seat. The July 9 draft · Transcript, speaker-tagged
JUN 26The Advisory Board voted 3-2 to accept the committee recommendation and submit it to the department, setting aside the controlled-substance number, the practicum and didactic hours, and the reciprocity timeline. Only Larry Leeman's "reluctant yes" is named on the record; the two votes against are not.
JUN 25The department presented a rules-style draft of the training subject to the committee. It introduced the module test-out mechanism that every later draft keeps, and the first reciprocity provision, which the June 12 recommendation did not contain. Superseded by the July 9 draft two weeks later. The June 25 draft
JUN 12The Training and Education Committee issued its recommendation, the origin document of this rulemaking: permit qualifications by role, curriculum modules, the 100 and 120 practicum hours with 20 supervisory hours, mentoring, record-keeping, and continuing education. The recommendation
JAN – MAYThe committee met five times to develop the recommendation: January 23, March 6, April 10, May 8 jointly with the End-of-Life committee, and May 22. The May 22 meeting was held but not recorded or posted by the department.
