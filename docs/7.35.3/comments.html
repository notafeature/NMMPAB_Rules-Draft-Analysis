New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Program · Advisory Board

Comments and the response

Updated November 15, 2026. The response proposes revisions to the text heard October 2; none is adopted.

On November 9, 2026, the New Mexico Department of Health proposed to strike the landlord statement and remove the certifying clinician from the medical psilocybin rule. Those were two of the changes the comments asked for most, in 54 and 60 of the 75 written comments. This page sets what the comments asked for beside what the department’s response to comments proposes, and states what the proposals would change.

1

2

What the proposed revisions would change

Each change below is proposed. The text in force on this site remains the August 25 publication until a revised or adopted text is published.

  • Patients who rent could be treated at home without asking a landlord to sign anything. Instead, the patient attests to having been informed of psilocybin’s legal status. Response, p. 4.
  • Getting into the program goes through the licensed practitioner, who screens the patient with a department tool. There is no separate visit to a prescriber with a controlled-substance number, and no in-person exam unless the patient is referred based on risk or medical need. A patient who has not seen a medical provider in 12 months would be referred to one first. Response, pp. 2 to 3.
  • Enrollment would be decided in 10 business days, not 30 days. Response, p. 1.
  • Practitioners would have a written list of qualifying licenses from the department, plus a way in for other licenses through a letter from their licensing board. Response, pp. 5 to 6.
  • Group sessions would require one practitioner per nine patients, plus one more practitioner, facilitator, or qualified student per three. Response, p. 5.

The route to each permit under the August 25 text is on Routes to a permit; the certifying clinician requirement the response proposes to remove is on The controlled-substance number.

3

Who commented

The 75 written comments came from 71 people and groups. 25 of them arrived in the last day and a half before comment closed. 28 people spoke at the hearing, whose recording runs 2 hours 27 minutes.

The department heard from patients, hospice nurses, therapists, physicians, researchers, veterans, and traditional healers. These organizations also wrote in:

  • End of Life Options New Mexico
  • Disability Rights New Mexico
  • The Psychedelic Bar Association’s New Mexico Working Group
  • The Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS)
  • Healing Advocacy, the Psychedelic Mental Health Access Alliance, and Rudick Law Group
  • Firefighters for Plant Medicine

Advisory Board members wrote in too. Senator Jeff Steinborn, the Medical Psilocybin Act’s primary author, told the hearing: “I am totally opposed to the landlord sign-off for homebound patients” (recording, 1:33:12).

Many of the comments used shared wording and added their own reasons around it: 42 of the 75 share at least one sentence word for word. The exemption sentence many of them carry is the language End of Life Options New Mexico proposed in its letter, Exhibit 8.GG.

4

Positions the department held

The department declined to reduce the practicum hours. It also kept practitioner supervision of facilitators, the 90-day limit on other approved locations, and two wilderness-trained staff for remote sessions. Response, pp. 4 and 6.

The response does not answer some requests:

  • Whether the practitioner’s screening, and preparation and integration sessions, can happen by telehealth. The in-person exam rule is set to go, but the response does not say how screening is done instead.
  • Registered nurses as practitioners.
  • A path for traditional and Indigenous healers to serve as practitioners or facilitators.
  • Staffing ratios set by dose, or exceptions for end-of-life care.
  • The proposed amendments to 7.35.2 NMAC, which still define the certifying clinician with a controlled-substance number.

5

What happens next

These changes are proposed, not final. They take effect only when the department adopts the rule and publishes it in the New Mexico Register. Until then, the August 25 text is the one on the table.

August 25, 2026

Revised proposed ruleCurrent state of truth

The revised proposed rule, published August 25 with the notice fixing the October 2 hearing, superseding the set-aside July 23 text. Raised the practitioner and facilitator therapy module from 30 didactic hours to 65, at least one third in person, and the simulated patient requirement from 5 hours to 10. Added eleven curriculum topics without per-area hour minimums, a case-presentation evaluation inside the unchanged 100 and 120 practicum hours, a low-risk requirement on the first 20 administration-day hours, and a didactic waiver. Kept the role names certifying clinician and practitioner, defining them in amendments to 7.35.2.7 published the same day.

This document supersedes four earlier ones. Each of them, what it is, what superseded it and when, and a download, is in the register at Meetings and filings.
