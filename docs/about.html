How this site is built

Updated July 25, 2026.

What this site is, where every statement comes from, what was produced with AI assistance, and how to report an error.

Read this before relying on anything here

This site is a community reference, not an official source. It is not affiliated with the New Mexico Department of Health or the Medical Psilocybin Advisory Board. Nothing here is final, promulgated rule text, and nothing here is legal or medical advice.

It was built with AI assistance. AI tools handled transcription cleanup, quote extraction, drafting, and cross-checking against the public record, with human review of the result. The meeting transcripts it relies on are unofficial auto-transcripts with speakers inferred from context. Errors of transcription, attribution, or interpretation are possible. Where this site and a primary source disagree, the primary source is right.

Method

Where the content comes from

Every page is built from the public record of the Training and Education rulemaking, in this order of authority:

  • Source documents. The current source for every requirement on this site is the July 23, 2026 published proposed rule (7.35.3), the text going to the August 28 hearing. Three earlier documents are history and are cited only where a page compares versions: the committee's June 12 recommendation, the department's June 25 draft, and the July 9 board-meeting draft. Draft language is quoted verbatim from these PDFs and every quote links to its source page. All of them, with what each one superseded, are on the documents page.
  • Meeting transcripts. Full transcripts of the June 25 and June 26 committee meetings, the July 9 Advisory Board meeting, and both July 17 meetings. These are unofficial auto-transcripts. The June 25, June 26 and July 9 transcripts are speaker-tagged, with speakers reconstructed from roll-call and hand-off cues. The two July 17 transcripts carry no speaker labels at all, and none have been added: where this site names a speaker from those two, the speaker is fixed by the surrounding text and the basis is recorded. Names garbled by transcription are corrected: Zurlo, Peskuski, Leeman, Dezbaá, Fatemi. Where a speaker or a vote could not be identified, it is left unattributed, including the two votes against the June 26 motion.
  • Live meetings, transcript pending. Statements from the July 16 End-of-Life Care committee meeting, the July 17 Advisory Board meeting (including the revised certifying-clinician language on the CS-number page), and the July 17 Training and Education Committee meeting (unofficial notes logged live on Training hours) are recorded from the live meetings. No transcript or updated draft document has been posted yet; these statements sit in the middle "Transcript" tier below until one is, and will be checked against the posted record when it appears.
  • Out-of-state benchmarks. Comparisons (Oregon, Colorado, Washington D.C., CIIS, the buprenorphine waiver) cite the primary rule or statute for each row, linked at the point of use.

Accuracy

How verified each kind of statement is

Verbatim

Draft-rule and recommendation quotes are copied from the source PDFs and can be checked one click away. Strongest tier.

Transcript

Meeting quotes and outcomes come from the unofficial transcripts. Wording is faithful to the auto-transcript, which itself may contain errors; speaker attribution is inferred.

Compiled

Benchmarks and background are sourced to the cited primary document but were collected and summarized with AI assistance; a citation error is possible.

Known soft points are flagged in place rather than smoothed over. One stands right now. The June 26 motion is attributed to Brenda Burgard per the meeting-note summary, because the raw transcript is unlabeled at that segment.

Policy

What this site will and will not do

  • It records dated events and attributed quotes. It does not characterize how people felt or what they meant.
  • It explains facts and distinctions. It does not recommend outcomes, and no page argues a position.
  • When the record changes, pages are updated and the revision is noted on the page.

Corrections

Report an error

If anything here is wrong, misattributed, or out of date, send it through the community input form with the topic "A correction to this site." Corrections are checked against the primary source and fixed on the page, with the revision noted.

Provenance: the document chain and this page’s revisions▸

The document chain, oldest first

  1. June 12, 2026

    Committee recommendation

    The Training and Education Committee's own recommendation. Origin of the 100 and 120 practicum hours and the 20 supervisory hours. The Advisory Board voted 3-2 on June 26 to accept it, setting three items aside.

  2. June 25, 2026

    Department draft

    The department's first rules-style draft of the same subject. Introduced the module test-out mechanism that every later draft keeps, and the first reciprocity provision, which the June 12 recommendation did not contain.

  3. July 9, 2026

    Board-meeting draft

    The draft the Advisory Board went through section by section on July 9. Used placeholder section numbers shown as 7.35.3.X. Set the shared didactic module at 25 hours. Carried the older waiver dates of December 31, 2026 and June 30, 2027, which the board extended by vote that day without the text being updated.

  4. July 23, 2026

    Published proposed ruleCurrent state of truth

    The department's published proposed rule, and the text going to the August 28 hearing. First version with final section numbering, 7.35.3.1 through .28. Raised the shared didactic module from 25 hours to 30 and added three curriculum topics. Carried the 100 and 120 practicum hours forward unchanged, six days after the board voted 7-0 to send those hours back to committee. Wrote both waiver dates as December 31, 2027. Added a rule that the practicum may not begin before half the didactic and all simulated patient hours are complete.
