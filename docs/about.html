How this site is built

What this site is, where its facts come from, and how to correct it.

Updated August 24, 2026.

What this site is

An independent community record of New Mexico's Training and Education rulemaking for the medical psilocybin program, 7.35.3 NMAC. It exists so that a person affected by the rule can know, at any moment, what the text says, what is still being decided, and how it came to be this way, without attending a meeting or reading a transcript. It serves board and committee members, providers and educators, people preparing to comment at the hearing, and anyone following the process. When the rulemaking ends, it remains as the account of how the rule was made.

Method

Read this before relying on anything here. Quoted text is verbatim from the source, with line breaks collapsed to single spaces and nothing else altered. Every claim about what the rule requires cites the published text by section, and every claim about what was said or decided cites a named meeting transcript. Where a transcript carries no speaker labels, which is true of both July 17 transcripts, a speaker is named only where the surrounding text fixes the identity, and the basis is stated. Where this site and a primary source disagree, the primary source is right.

The site is built with AI assistance from the public meeting record and the published documents, and reviewed against the sources before publication. Unofficial transcripts are auto-generated and may contain errors; they are labeled as such wherever they are relied on.

Statements on this site occupy three strengths of verification, and each is only as strong as its tier.

VERBATIMRule and recommendation quotes are copied from the source PDFs and can be checked one step away. Strongest tier.
TRANSCRIPTMeeting quotes and outcomes come from the unofficial transcripts. Wording is faithful to the auto-transcript, which itself may contain errors, and speaker attribution can be inferred rather than labeled.
COMPILEDBenchmarks and background are sourced to the cited primary document but were collected and summarized with AI assistance; a citation error is possible.

Known soft points are flagged in place rather than smoothed over. One stands now: the June 26 motion is attributed to Brenda Burgard per the meeting-note summary, because the raw transcript is unlabeled at that segment.

Corrections

Errors are corrected in place, and the correction is recorded twice: in the revision log at the foot of the page it touches, and in the repository history behind this site, which is public. To report one, use the public comment page and choose "An error on this site."

The document this page is read from

August 25, 2026

Revised proposed ruleCurrent state of truth

The revised proposed rule, published August 25 with the notice fixing the October 2 hearing, superseding the set-aside July 23 text. Raised the practitioner and facilitator therapy module from 30 didactic hours to 65, at least one third in person, and the simulated patient requirement from 5 hours to 10. Added eleven curriculum topics without per-area hour minimums, a case-presentation evaluation inside the unchanged 100 and 120 practicum hours, a low-risk requirement on the first 20 administration-day hours, and a didactic waiver. Kept the role names certifying clinician and practitioner, defining them in amendments to 7.35.2.7 published the same day.

This document supersedes four earlier ones. Each of them, what it is, what superseded it and when, and a download, is in the register at Meetings and filings.
