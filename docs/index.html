New Mexico medical psilocybin program · 7.35.3 NMAC

Where things stand

Reviewed August 24, 2026. Every claim on this site cites the last published text, a named transcript, or a filed document.

New Mexico is writing the rule that governs how medical psilocybin providers are trained and certified. The Department of Health published a proposed text on July 23, 2026 and later set it aside with its August 28 hearing; revised rules are forthcoming, the department's stated date August 25, with the rule hearing anticipated for October 2. The training hours went to the Training and Education Committee, whose recommendation stands at its August 21 position and is in the department's hands. This site holds the last published text, the recommendation, and the record of how both came to be.

These pages cover provider training, not patient eligibility. The Medical Psilocybin Act sets the qualifying conditions for patients: PTSD, major treatment-resistant depression, substance use disorders, and end-of-life care. What the Act requires, and how a patient is certified, is on the controlled-substance number page.

Where things stand

OPENNo proposed rule is currently published. The July 23 publication and its August 28 hearing were set aside; the July 23 text remains on this site as the last published text. The department's stated date for publishing revised proposed rules is August 25, and the rule hearing is anticipated for early October, October 2 named at the August 21 committee meeting, virtual and in person; the final notice, in preparation with the hearing officer, will fix the date. Public comment continues after publication, through the hearing.
OPENThe training hours are the committee's to recommend and the department's to write. The recommendation stands at the August 21 position: 80 didactic hours with minimums in nine content areas, and a staged practicum of 114 hours for licensed providers or 102 for facilitators, closing with case presentation and consultation. The department's own proposal, shown beside it on August 21, also sets 80 didactic hours and reaches 120 or 100 practicum hours, structured differently. No vote was taken; the recommendation is in the department's hands for the revised rules.
DEFECTThe last published text carries five defect notes across four sections, each at the provision it sits in. Three provisions a practicum change reaches are flagged as not working as written on What a practicum change touches. Two of those three are among the five; the third, a conflict between the two waivers that set 40 contact hours, is analyzed there and carries no note in the rule.
SETTLEDThe certifying-clinician controlled-substance number, kept July 17, and the reciprocity deadlines, set to December 31, 2027 on July 9. Both stand in the last published text, and neither is changed by the August 21 recommendation.

The document this page is read from

July 23, 2026

Published proposed ruleCurrent state of truth

The department's proposed rule as published July 23, set aside with its August 28 hearing and standing as the last published text; the department's stated date for revised rules is August 25, with the hearing anticipated for early October. First version with final section numbering, 7.35.3.1 through .28. Raised the shared didactic module from 25 hours to 30 and added three curriculum topics. Carried the 100 and 120 practicum hours forward unchanged, six days after the board voted 7-0 to send those hours back to committee. Wrote both waiver dates as December 31, 2027. Added a rule that the practicum may not begin before half the didactic and all simulated patient hours are complete.

This document supersedes three earlier ones. Each of them, what it is, what superseded it and when, and a download, is in the register at Meetings and filings.
